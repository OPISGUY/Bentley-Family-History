# What comes next for the family stories

## The people we could still hear about first-hand

Roy and Margaret are the next priority. Their wedding and death dates are on the tree. What would bring their page to life is a story only someone who knew them could tell: where they met, what work they did, or what a visit to their home was like. A captioned photograph or a recollection with the teller’s name would give the family something it can preserve in its own words.

## A day at work in Marsden

For Charles, Ann, Elizabeth and John, the censuses tell us exactly what jobs they did. The next question is where. A named mill, quarry, employer or contemporary newspaper item tied to Binn or Carr Clough would let us tell more of their working day. [Read Ann’s story](stories/ann-at-carr-clough.html).

## A wartime story needs a name and a household

Thomas Herbert was of serving age in 1914. A free record connecting a service number to his address or a known relative would give us a basis for a military story. His service history remains open. [Read the newspaper and military notes](life-sources.html#where-the-military-and-newspaper-searches-led).
