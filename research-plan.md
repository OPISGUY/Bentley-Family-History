# Research plan

Records to search next, in priority order. Tick items off and note the result (with a citation)
in a dated file under `research/`.

_Last updated: 10 October 2026_

## 1. Settle whether there were two John Bentleys (most urgent)

A John Bentley of Shelf was buried on 1 August 1540, yet a will dated 30 June 1540 already calls
Agnes "the late wife of John Bentley". See
[source checks, section 3](research/2026-10-10-source-checks.md#3-agnes-the-late-wife-of-john-bentley-and-land-at-high-bentley-1540).

- [ ] Read William Brooke's original registered will: York probate register vol. 11, f. 427
      (Borthwick Institute for Archives, University of York). Confirm the wording "late wife of
      John Bentley" and the date of 30 June 1540.
- [ ] Check the original Halifax register entry for the 1 August 1540 burial (West Yorkshire
      Archive Service), in case the printed edition left out a detail such as a father's name.
- [ ] Search the York probate indexes for a John Bentley of Shelf or Halifax, c. 1530–1541.
      *Halifax Wills* (to 1544) prints none, but an administration or a lost will may still be
      indexed.
- [ ] Find the record behind Laurence Bentley's 1496 fine for land in Shelf (probably a manor
      court roll). The same court should record later Bentley deaths and heirs taking up land,
      which could name John's heir and show when he died.

## 2. Check the Ancestry sources

- [ ] List every source cited on John G. Bentley's Ancestry profile, especially for the 1504
      birth, the 1 August 1540 death, his parents and his wife.
- [ ] Find where the "G." initial and the name "Waterhouse" first appear.
- [ ] Trace the Monmouth link back to its first appearance: a published pedigree, or another
      online tree?

## 3. Agnes

- [ ] Look for a Waterhouse will (c. 1500–1545) naming a daughter Agnes married to a Bentley.
      *Halifax Wills* Parts I and II are the place to start.
- [ ] Follow Agnes Bentley, widow, who married Edward Berstowe on 20 November 1542. Do any later
      Berstowe/Bairstow wills name Bentley stepchildren?

## 4. High Bentley Hall

- [ ] Arthur Bentley "of High Bentley Hall" in 1537: find the William Thorpe charity deed and
      any other record of Arthur. Work out how he relates to John.
- [ ] Read J. Lister's article in *Transactions of the Halifax Antiquarian Society* (1905), cited
      in the listing.
- [ ] Read the books on the house listed by Calderdale Companion: *Aisled Houses in the Halifax
      Area*, *Ancient Halls in & about Halifax* and *The Old Halls & Manor Houses of Yorkshire*.
- [ ] Find when and how the hall passed from the Bentleys to the Wade family (Richard Wade, 1661).

## 5. Earlier Yorkshire generations

- [ ] Identify the 1296 record of the Bentley family of Shelf.
- [ ] Read the 1379 poll tax return for Shelf (Thomas de Bentley) in the original or a published
      transcription.
- [ ] Read every Bentley entry in *Halifax Wills* Part I (1389–1514) for Shelf connections.
- [ ] Check Joseph Foster, *Pedigrees of the County Families of Yorkshire* (1874), for a Bentley
      pedigree and its sources.

## 6. Monmouth

Only once the Yorkshire line is documented.

- [ ] Search Monmouthshire and Welsh records for a Sir John Bentley, c. 1470–1527.
- [ ] Check Isabel Brookhouse (1473–1503) and James Bentley (b. 1419) the same way.

## 7. The link to the present day

The tree also needs a documented chain from you back to the 1500s. Working backwards one
generation at a time is the most reliable way to prove that you descend from the High Bentley
family.

- [ ] Write down what is already known for each generation back from your grandfather: names,
      dates, places, and the source for each.
- [ ] Confirm where your grandfather's land was (the notes mention Slaithwaite, Marsden and
      Saddleworth, which are three different places) and find the deed or Land Registry record of
      the sale.
- [ ] Use civil registration (from 1837), censuses (1841–1921) and parish registers to take each
      generation back towards the 1500s.
