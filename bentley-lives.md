# The lives of the Bentleys

In 1871, the census taker at Binn put three different jobs against three names in one Bentley household. Charles dug stone. His daughter Elizabeth wound bobbins. His son John, aged ten, worked as a woollen piecer. A generation later, John was still earning his living from cloth. To see where that thread begins, we have to go back to Saddleworth.

## Mathew and his daughter An

On 1 May 1613, someone recording a baptism at St Chad’s, Saddleworth, wrote down a girl’s name as **An** and her father’s as **Mathew Bentley**. Nineteen months later, on 2 December 1614, the register names a boy baptised Mathew, son of Mathew. Those two entries are our earliest glimpse of a Bentley family in the district. An is the first child we can call by name. Her father was already a parent when he first appeared in the surviving records. [An’s baptism](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4b38865574817e6e3407/an-bentley-baptism-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1613-05-01) · [Mathew’s baptism](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4b3e865574817e6e34c2/mathew-bentley-baptism-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1614-12-02).

A few years later, news of violence in Saddleworth reached Manchester. An entry dated 22 September in the constables’ accounts for 1619–20 records a hue and cry for Henry Platte after he wounded a Mathew Bentley of Saddleworth. The trail ran far enough for constables in another town to record it. The account gives us the injured man’s name and home, but too little to identify him as An’s father. It also leaves our original family question open: what brought the Bentleys to Saddleworth in the first place? [Manchester constables’ accounts, printed page 69](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Cornell_University_Library_digitization_%28IA_cu31924091777437%29.pdf#page=95).

## Jonas and the two Matthews

In 1654 the register gives Jonas Bentley’s son John both a birth date, 16 August, and a baptism date, 27 August. Ten years later it names Jonas again when his son Mathew was baptised on 11 December 1664. The surviving entries let us see Jonas as a father on two particular days a decade apart. His relationship to An’s father remains open. [The two baptism transcriptions](research/2026-10-10-the-two-breaks.md).

Another entry records the burial of Ellin, wife of a Mathew, on 5 May 1719. In February 1735/6 the clerk recorded the burial of a man of Stone-edge remembered as “old Matthew”. The distinction mattered locally: another Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge lived until 1751. The younger man’s occupation and household are unusually well recorded. Identifying the older man with Jonas’s son is plausible. Making him the younger man’s father is a separate, unresolved step. [Ellin’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e02865574817e6fa41c/elline-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1719-05-05) · [Old Matthew’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e66865574817e6fc59a/matthew-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1736-02-26).

## Matthew and Sarah in the cloth country

Dean-head is the home named when Matthew and Sarah’s son James was baptised on 25 March 1722. Their daughter Mary’s entry gives Knowll, their daughter Sarah’s gives Stone-edge, and Helen’s gives Knar. Ten children appear across more than twenty years of baptisms. The changing place names bring this family closer than a modern county label can, though the entries alone cannot tell us how often they moved. [Matthew and Sarah’s household and individual parish entries](research/2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md#4-matthew-bentley-of-standedge-c16921751-and-sarah).

Matthew’s burial on 20 November 1751 still identifies him by his trade: clothier. Woollen manufacture in this part of Saddleworth could be carried on alongside farming, and cloth could go to market in Huddersfield by packhorse. That gives the hills an economic purpose which is easy to miss today. The register supplies Matthew’s trade. His own landholding, customers and working arrangements remain unknown. [Matthew’s burial entry](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e99865574817e6fd8fa/matthew-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1751-11-20) · [Local historical account and sources](research/2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md#why-live-in-that-landscape).

## James and Mary marry at Christmas

James Bentley and Mary Nield married in Saddleworth on **26 December 1745**. When their daughter Sarah was baptised in 1748 and their son John in 1750, the family’s home was recorded as Barn. By Esther’s baptism in 1753 the clerk was writing Stone-edge. James appears as a clothier, as his father Matthew had before him. We can follow this couple for more than fifteen years through the names, homes and occupation recorded at their children’s baptisms. [James and Mary’s marriage](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4f8e865574817e70324a/james-bentley-mary-nield-marriage-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1745-12-26) · [Their children’s entries](family-groups.md#8-james-bentley-and-mary-nield).

## Helen and the stone at St Chad’s

James’s sister Helen makes a branch of the tree unusually easy to picture. In December 1752, the baptism of her daughter Sarah names Helen of Stand-edge and Thomas Platt, a clothier of Lee. Helen and Thomas married the following January. Later entries put their household at Heyhouse. L. N. Kidd’s account follows the couple through these places and records where they were eventually commemorated together. [Sarah’s baptism and the family records](research/2026-11-04-wider-family.md) · [Kidd’s account, printed pages 3–4](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=6).

Helen and Thomas’s last summer together can be dated almost day by day. Their son Joseph was baptised on **24 August 1769**. Kidd reports that Helen died on **1 September**, in her thirty-sixth year, and was buried at St Chad’s on **9 September**. Joseph’s burial followed on **17 November**. Thomas lived another twenty years. Kidd places their grave in the old churchyard at Saddleworth and says its inscription identifies Thomas with Whams Inn. The dates tell us how close these losses were. The account gives no cause of death for either Helen or Joseph. [Burial account and memorial description, printed page 4](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=7).

## Hannah and the journey to Liverpool

Helen’s granddaughter Hannah Platt was baptised at Friarmere in 1788. Her father James farmed and manufactured cotton. Hannah married John Gartside at Rochdale on 21 May 1810. He was a bookkeeper who had been baptised as an adult in the river Roche and went on to serve as a Baptist deacon. The couple lived first at Haugh and later at Hamer, near Rochdale. Kidd’s account gives this branch of the family a particular texture: it takes us from a Saddleworth clothier’s granddaughter to a household where keeping accounts and chapel life mattered. [Kidd’s account, printed pages 4–5](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=7).

Kidd reports that Hannah died of cholera in Liverpool on **6 August 1833**, aged 45. She was buried at **St James’ Mount in Liverpool** and remembered on a gravestone back at Ogden. Those two places tell different parts of her story. Liverpool was where her life ended, and Ogden was where people continued to commemorate her. What took her to Liverpool remains unknown. The burial and cause of death come from Kidd’s published account. [Hannah’s death and burial, printed page 5](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=8).

## John and Ann give a name again

John Bentley married Ann Broadbent in 1771. In August 1776 they baptised a son called John. His burial came two days later. In August 1777 another son called John was baptised and buried two days apart. A William baptised in July 1779 was also buried two days later. A further son John appears in 1781 and a William in 1788. It is easy to read these repeating names as duplicate entries. Read as baptisms and burials together, they preserve separate children whose lives might otherwise disappear from the tree. [Dated baptisms and burials](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

John worked as a clothier. His own burial, under the spelling Bently, took place on 15 November 1789. The youngest William had been baptised only nineteen months earlier. Whether that child grew up to be Betty’s husband is the gap between this household and the next. [John’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4f22865574817e700f20/john-bently-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1789-11-15).

## Betty and the four weavers at Carr Clough

Charles Bentley’s 1828 baptism names William and Betty and places the family at Standage. William was a clothier. Charles’s brother Wright was baptised three years later, when the family’s address was Coat Hill. By 1851 Betty was a widow at Carr Clough in Marsden. The census lists her sons John, William, Charles and Wright. **All four gave the same occupation: woollen weaver.** Their sister Ann was eighteen. [Baptism transcriptions](research/2026-11-04-charles-william-review.md) · [1851 census, HO 107/2291](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/6611c6f8f493fd652b692ca6/charles-bentley-1851-yorkshire-west-riding-almondbury-1828-).

That census catches the brothers together before their lives diverged. Charles raised his family in Marsden. Death registrations at St Kilda in Australia fit three of his brothers: William in 1891, Wright in 1902 and Joseph in 1907. Wright’s entry names his mother as Betty Bottomley. It is a small detail, but it ties an Australian record back to the woman at the head of the Carr Clough household half a century earlier. Their ships, departure dates and reasons for leaving remain unknown. [The St Kilda records](research/2026-11-05-australian-households.md).

## Charles leaves weaving for stone

In 1861 Charles and Sarah Dyson were still at Carr Clough. Three-month-old John was at home with his sisters Elizabeth and Rebecca. Charles and his adult sister Ann both gave woollen weaving as their occupation. Ann’s presence matters: the family’s textile work can be followed through a woman as well as through her brothers. [1861 census, RG 9/3243](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906c0a3e9379091b1376b45/john-bentley-1861-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-).

Ten years later Charles and Sarah were at Binn. Charles was now a **stone delver**, someone who dug or quarried stone. The enumerator listed fifteen-year-old Elizabeth as a bobbin winder and ten-year-old John as a woollen piecer, a job joining broken threads. In the space of two censuses, the father had changed both his home and his trade. Two of his children had gone into textiles. The record gives neither a quarry nor a mill name, but it tells us who was already working and how young they were. [1871 census, RG 10/4340](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906ed93e9379091b1751b6a/john-bentley-1871-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-).

The proposal concerning the Wessenden reservoir road and Binn Lane appeared on 18 November 1870. It locates the works being proposed in Charles’s neighbourhood. His employer and quarry are unknown. [Original notice, page 4969](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/23680/page/4969/data.pdf).

## John the piecer becomes a weaver

Twenty years after the census counted John at work aged ten, another enumerator came to Binn. John was thirty, married to Sarah Ann Haigh, and employed as a woollen weaver. At home were Willie, six, Sarah H., four, Thomas Herbert, two, and Sam, one. There was also Dan Haigh, aged seventy-one, recorded as an uncle living on his own means. Three generations shared the address. Set the two censuses beside each other and the ten-year-old at work comes back into view as the father of four young children. [1891 census, RG 12/3554](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/590706c3e9379091b1941bc2/thomas-h-bentley-1891-yorkshire-west-riding-huddersfield-1889-).

Sarah Ann can also be found in the memorial index for **St Bartholomew’s, Marsden**. The inscription identifies her as John’s wife and as the daughter of Thomas and Harriet Haigh. It gives 10 August 1934 as her death date and her age as 77. An adjoining John Bentley entry gives 10 November 1918 and an age of 67. Our John’s census ages point to 57. Sarah Ann’s memorial is identifiable. The John entry needs corroboration before we can say it marks her husband’s burial. [Marsden History Group memorial index, printed page 7](https://www.marsdenhistory.co.uk/downloads/graveyard_index.pdf#page=8).

Two years before the 1891 census, Dyson and Co. advertised a change in its partnership at Upper Bank Bottom Mill, Marsden. This identifies a local business but gives no employee list. John’s mill is still unknown. [Original notice, 29 November 1889, page 6867](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/25997/page/6867/data.pdf).

## Thomas Herbert and Lauretta in the local paper

Thomas Herbert was the two-year-old at Binn in 1891. His marriage to Lauretta Jones was registered in Huddersfield in the first quarter of 1920. Forty-three years later the couple appear together again, this time in the *Colne Valley Guardian*. A death notice published on **27 September 1963**, page 8, names Lauretta as Thomas Herbert’s wife and reports that she died on **25 September**, aged 75. That named relationship helps distinguish her from people with similar names and gives September precedence over a July date passed down in the family. The available newspaper extract is machine-read. The address is too garbled to publish with confidence. [Newspaper reference and evidence](research/2026-11-04-wider-family.md) · [Marriage and census references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

Thomas Herbert was about twenty-five when the First World War began. I could not verify a service number, regiment or military record linked to his Marsden household. A newspaper search illustrates the risk of filling in that gap from a name alone. An apparent *London Gazette* hit for his full name leads to a 1905 page that lists a Herbert Bentley of Bingley among temporary assistant postmen and a separate Thomas Herbert elsewhere on the page. The printed notice gives us no job or service history for Lauretta’s husband. [Original Gazette page, 1 August 1905](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/27823/page/5301/data.pdf).

## Roy and Margaret in postwar Yorkshire

Roy brings the story back within family memory. His birth was registered at Huddersfield in spring 1930, with Jones given as his mother’s maiden name. His father Thomas Herbert had spent his early childhood at Binn. Roy married Margaret Cartwright in the autumn of 1953, in the Upper Agbrigg registration district. The two marriage entries point to each other through their surnames and share the same reference, volume 2d, page 989. That gives their marriage a firmer date than we currently have for Roy’s work or any National Service. The family reports that Roy later married Betty and Margaret also remarried. Margaret died before the reporting relative was born. That relative was six when Roy died at Fartown in 1998. The 1991 death entry under Bentley and the exact birth date taken from it remain unconfirmed. [Roy and Margaret’s civil registration references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

## The road below the reservoir

Family recollection supplied 9 November 2026. Thomas Herbert’s great-grandchild identifies his place of death as a terrace on Binn Road below the reservoir, recalls the family move with Roy to a Slaithwaite farm, and suggests Roy carried it on and Thomas moved back. The precise holding, tenure and dates are open. The same contributor remembers visiting a maternal relative further up Binn Lane as a child in the 1990s and walking around Butterley and Wessenden for years before learning the Bentley connection. The two houses are distinct in this account. The published story keeps the relative and house unidentified.

The census transcriptions say Binn in 1871 and 1891. They establish three generations in the locality across the two returns. Thomas’s death registration establishes the district and quarter. The terrace detail comes from the family. [1871 household](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906ed93e9379091b1751b6a/john-bentley-1871-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-) · [1891 household](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/590706c3e9379091b1941bc2/thomas-h-bentley-1891-yorkshire-west-riding-huddersfield-1889-) · [Civil registration references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

## Where the military and newspaper searches led

The Marsden Park memorial covers both world wars and St Bartholomew’s has a First World War parishioners’ memorial. The published transcription of the parish tablet lists the fallen. No Bentley appears in that transcription. Thomas Herbert survived the war, so this tablet cannot settle whether he served. I could not verify a named Bentley in our households from those listings. The Imperial War Museums service search and National Archives catalogue returned no usable individual record in this search. Service remains an open question, including for men who survived the war. [Marsden Park memorial](https://www.warmemorialsonline.org.uk/memorial/138580/) · [Parish memorial](https://www.warmemorialsonline.org.uk/memorial/156214/).

Wright is an unusual first name, but even here a newspaper hit needs care. An 1849 notice names **Wright Bentley** in an Oldham iron and brass business with John Bentley and James Booth. Later notices record an Oldham ironfounder’s partnership dissolution and a bankruptcy petition. Our Wright appears as a woollen weaver with Betty at Carr Clough in 1851. I found no address, relative or other identifying detail joining him to the ironfounder. Those business events therefore belong to the Oldham man’s history until his identity can be established. [1849 notice](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/21011/page/2630/data.pdf) · [1850 notice](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/21114/page/1929/data.pdf) · [Bankruptcy proceedings](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/21649/page/115/data.pdf).

The *Guardian* notice is the press item linked to a family member here. The full British Newspaper Archive image and Australian Trove newspaper results were unavailable in this search.
