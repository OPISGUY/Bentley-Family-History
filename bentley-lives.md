# The lives of the Bentleys

In 1871, the census taker at Binn put three different jobs against three names in one Bentley household. Charles dug stone. His daughter Elizabeth wound bobbins. His son John, aged ten, worked as a woollen piecer. A generation later, John was still earning his living from cloth. To see where that thread begins, we have to go back to Saddleworth.

## Mathew and his daughter An

On 1 May 1613, someone recording a baptism at St Chad’s, Saddleworth, wrote down a girl’s name as **An** and her father’s as **Mathew Bentley**. Nineteen months later, on 2 December 1614, the register names a boy baptised Mathew, son of Mathew. Those two entries are our earliest glimpse of a Bentley family in the district. An is the first child we can call by name. Her father was already a parent when he first appeared in the surviving records. [An’s baptism](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4b38865574817e6e3407/an-bentley-baptism-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1613-05-01) · [Mathew’s baptism](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4b3e865574817e6e34c2/mathew-bentley-baptism-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1614-12-02).

A few years later, news of violence in Saddleworth reached Manchester. An entry dated 22 September in the constables’ accounts for 1619–20 records a hue and cry for Henry Platte after he wounded a Mathew Bentley of Saddleworth “that he is in great danger of death”. Nineteen years after that, at the West Riding Quarter Sessions in **January 1638/9**, a **Mathewe Bentley, late of Saddleworth, labourer**, was ordered committed to **York Castle** for refusing to become bound with sureties to appear at the next sessions and keep the peace (*SHS Bulletin* 21(1), Spring 1991, p. 12). When the **February 1641/2 Saddleworth Protestation Return** listed the adult men of the chapelry three years later (Radcliffe 1887, Appendix, pp. 483–487), **no Bentley appeared on the roll**, before **“Mathey” Bentley** was buried at St Chad’s on **8 February 1657/8**. Those legal and tax records give us the injured man’s and prisoner’s name, trade and locality, and offer a plausible reason why no Bentley signed the 1641/2 Protestation, though they do not prove whether the 1619–20 or 1638/9 records refer to the 1613 father, the 1614 son, or another namesake. [Manchester constables’ accounts, printed page 69](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Cornell_University_Library_digitization_%28IA_cu31924091777437%29.pdf#page=95) · [1638/9 York Castle committal and 1612–42 records](research/2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md#was-he-running-from-something) · [1641/2 Protestation and 1664–74 Hearth Tax checks](research/2026-11-11-standedge-to-1600s-and-beyond.md#41-why-no-bentley-appears-in-the-16412-protestation-return-or-the-16641674-hearth-tax).

## Jonas and the two Matthews

In 1654 the register gives Jonas Bentley’s son John both a birth date, 16 August, and a baptism date, 27 August. Ten years later it names Jonas again when his son Mathew was baptised on 11 December 1664. When Jonas was buried on 6 July 1686 (as Jonah Bentley), an inventory of his estate dated **18 August 1686** described him explicitly as **Jonas Bentley of Standedge, Saddleworth** (`H/KID`, p. 248)—placing the family at Standedge forty years before the parish registers first name the farm. Neither the 1641/2 Saddleworth Protestation Return nor the 1664–1674 chargeable Hearth Tax lists a Bentley, suggesting that the 1613–14 household was absent or unrecorded in 1641/2 (three years after Mathewe Bentley of Saddleworth was ordered committed to York Castle in January 1638/9) and that Jonas held at Standedge as an exempt smallholder or under-tenant. Meanwhile, **John Bentley of Diggle, carpenter** (inventory **18 February 1713/14**, `H/KID`, p. 248; WYAS Leeds `ACC 2433`), fits Jonas’s 1654 son John in the hamlet immediately next to Standedge; less than five months after his inventory, on **3/10 July 1714** at St Chad, Rochdale and St Chad, Saddleworth, **Mary Bentley of Saddleworth** married **by Licence** to **James Bradbury of Fairbanks, Diggle, clothier** (c.1689–1737; Mary buried 3 December 1764), naming their firstborn son **John Bradbury** (1715) and linking the Diggle/Standedge Bentleys into the Bradbury and Broadbent families of Diggle (`Supported`). By contrast, **John Bentley of Boarshurst, yeoman** (active 1703–1750; joint tenant of Frenches Fulling Mill in 1719; will proved 12 May 1750; named with his mother Elizabeth on the 13 June 1750 Saddleworth Manor Court Roll) headed a separate documented branch at Boarshurst. [The two baptism transcriptions](research/2026-10-10-the-two-breaks.md) · [11 November Standedge-to-1600s review](research/2026-11-11-standedge-to-1600s-and-beyond.md).

Another entry records the burial of Ellin, wife of a Mathew, on 5 May 1719. In February 1735/6 the clerk recorded the burial of a man of Stone-edge remembered as “old Matthew”. The distinction mattered locally: another Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge lived until 1751. Although no single baptism survives from the gap-ridden 1690s to prove that the younger Matthew was old Matthew’s son, four pieces of evidence now converge: both men lived at Standedge (where Jonas had already lived in 1686); Matthew and Sarah named a daughter **Helen / Ellin** (after old Matthew’s wife **Ellin**, d. 1719); they named children **John** (1728/9) and **Esther** (1731), matching the only two named children baptised to Mathew in 1700–1703 (**Ester** 1700 and **John** 1702/3); and the very first son born to Matthew and Sarah after old Matthew’s burial in February 1735/6 was named **Matthew** (January 1738/9). [Ellin’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e02865574817e6fa41c/elline-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1719-05-05) · [Old Matthew’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e66865574817e6fc59a/matthew-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1736-02-26) · [Four-point correlation](research/2026-11-11-standedge-to-1600s-and-beyond.md#1-jonas-bentley-at-standedge-in-1686-and-the-four-point-correlation-to-matthew-of-standedge-d-1751).

## Matthew and Sarah in the cloth country

Dean-head is the home named when Matthew and Sarah’s son James was baptised on 25 March 1722. Their daughter Mary’s entry in January 1723/4 gives Knowll, their daughter Sarah’s in 1726 gives Stone-edge, and Helen’s in 1733/4 gives Knar. Clara Shaw’s Knowl gazetteer (*SHS Bulletin* 12(1), Spring 1982, p. 14) notes their move from Deanhead to Knowl and Standedge (and records an unplaced Martha Bentley, spinster of Knowl, whose daughter Mary was baptised on 14 July 1744), while the Acre gazetteer (*SHS Bulletin* 13(1), Spring 1983, p. 15) places **Abraham Bentley, clothier**, at **Acre** immediately below Deanhead in 1750 before his burial from Standedge in 1792 aged 84. Ten children of Matthew and Sarah appear across more than twenty years of baptisms. [Matthew and Sarah’s household and individual parish entries](research/2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md#4-matthew-bentley-of-standedge-c16921751-and-sarah) · [Knowl and Acre gazetteers](research/2026-11-11-standedge-to-1600s-and-beyond.md#2-matthew-and-sarah-bentley-at-deanhead-knowl-and-standedge-and-helen-ellin-platts-1769-gravestone).

Matthew’s will was dated 18 November 1751 (proved 3 March 1753, `H/KID`, p. 248) and his burial on 20 November 1751 identifies him by his trade: clothier. Woollen manufacture in this part of Saddleworth could be carried on alongside farming, and cloth could go to market in Huddersfield by packhorse. That gives the hills an economic purpose which is easy to miss today. The register supplies Matthew’s trade. His own landholding, customers and working arrangements remain unknown. [Matthew’s burial entry](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4e99865574817e6fd8fa/matthew-bentley-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1751-11-20) · [Local historical account and sources](research/2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md#why-live-in-that-landscape).

## James and Mary marry at Christmas

James Bentley and Mary Nield married in Saddleworth on **26 December 1745**. When their daughter Sarah was baptised in 1748 and their son John in 1750, the family’s home was recorded as Barn. By Esther’s baptism in 1753 the clerk was writing Stone-edge. James appears as a clothier, as his father Matthew had before him. We can follow this couple for more than fifteen years through the names, homes and occupation recorded at their children’s baptisms. [James and Mary’s marriage](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4f8e865574817e70324a/james-bentley-mary-nield-marriage-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1745-12-26) · [Their children’s entries](family-groups.md#8-james-bentley-and-mary-nield).

## Helen and the stone at St Chad’s

James’s sister Helen makes a branch of the tree unusually easy to picture. In December 1752, the baptism of her daughter Sarah names Helen of Stand-edge and Thomas Platt, a clothier of Lee. Helen and Thomas married the following January. Later entries put their household at Heyhouse. L. N. Kidd’s account follows the couple through these places and records where they were eventually commemorated together. [Sarah’s baptism and the family records](research/2026-11-04-wider-family.md) · [Kidd’s account, printed pages 3–4](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=6).

Helen and Thomas’s last summer together can be dated almost day by day. Their son Joseph was baptised on **24 August 1769**. Kidd reports that Helen died on **1 September**, in her thirty-sixth year, and was buried at St Chad’s on **9 September**. Joseph’s burial followed on **17 November**. Thomas lived another twenty years. Kidd places their grave in the old churchyard at Saddleworth and says its inscription identifies Thomas with Whams Inn. The dates tell us how close these losses were. The account gives no cause of death for either Helen or Joseph. [Burial account and memorial description, printed page 4](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=7).

## Hannah and the journey to Liverpool

Helen’s granddaughter Hannah Platt was baptised at Friarmere in 1788. Her father James farmed and manufactured cotton. Hannah married John Gartside at Rochdale on 21 May 1810. He was a bookkeeper who had been baptised as an adult in the river Roche and went on to serve as a Baptist deacon. The couple lived first at Haugh and later at Hamer, near Rochdale. Kidd’s account gives this branch of the family a particular texture: it takes us from a Saddleworth clothier’s granddaughter to a household where keeping accounts and chapel life mattered. [Kidd’s account, printed pages 4–5](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=7).

Kidd reports that Hannah died of cholera in Liverpool on **6 August 1833**, aged 45. She was buried at **St James’ Mount in Liverpool** and remembered on a gravestone back at Ogden. Those two places tell different parts of her story. Liverpool was where her life ended, and Ogden was where people continued to commemorate her. What took her to Liverpool remains unknown. The burial and cause of death come from Kidd’s published account. [Hannah’s death and burial, printed page 5](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=8).

## John and Ann give a name again

John Bentley married Ann Broadbent in 1771. In August 1776 they baptised a son called John. His burial came two days later. In August 1777 another son called John was baptised and buried two days apart. A William baptised in July 1779 was also buried two days later. A further son John appears in 1781 and a William in 1788. It is easy to read these repeating names as duplicate entries. Read as baptisms and burials together, they preserve separate children whose lives might otherwise disappear from the tree. [Dated baptisms and burials](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

John worked as a clothier. His own burial, under the spelling Bently, took place on 15 November 1789. Two years later, on **26 November 1791** (`D-AAB/1/1–1/2`), **James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothier**, and **John Bentley of Bentleys, clothier**, purchased the freehold of the tenement called **"Bentleys"** from the lord of the manor, selling it in May 1806 (`D-AAB/1/5`); the **1822 Map of Saddleworth** (*SHS Bulletin* 29(1), Spring 1999, p. 8) marks **BENTLEYS** and **STANEDGE FOOT** at the foot of the Standedge escarpment, separate from **Bleak Hey Nook** lower down the hill where Grade II listed Bentley Farmhouse stands. On that exact same Friday—**26 November 1791**—a *Manchester Mercury* auction notice (*SHS Bulletin* 22(2), Summer 1992, p. 22) offered the two-dwelling farmhouse called **Woodmans**, jointly occupied by **Matthew Bentley** (matching the 1738/9 uncle) and **Thomas Rhodes** (named at St Chad’s on 4 April 1779 as the father of niece **Esther Bentley of Standedge’s** son William). Meanwhile, John and Ann’s youngest son William had been baptised only nineteen months before his father’s death. On **Sunday, 6 June 1819**, **William and Betty Bentley of Standage, clothier**, and **James and Ann Bentley of Standage, clothier**, brought their sons **William** and **Charles** to be baptised at St Chad’s on the exact same day—strongly supporting the identification of Betty’s husband William and Ann’s husband James with the surviving brothers baptised in 1788 and 1784. [John’s burial](https://www.freereg.org.uk/search_records/685e4f22865574817e700f20/john-bently-burial-yorkshire-west-riding-saddleworth-1789-11-15) · [1822 map, Woodmans auction and 6 June 1819 double baptism](research/2026-11-11-standedge-to-1600s-and-beyond.md#3-resolving-the-bentleys-tenement-17911822-and-the-6-june-1819-double-baptism-at-st-chads).

## Betty and the four weavers at Carr Clough

Charles Bentley’s 1828 baptism names William and Betty and places the family at Standage. William was a clothier. Charles’s brother Wright was baptised three years later, when the family’s address was Coat Hill. By 1851 Betty was a widow at Carr Clough in Marsden. The census lists her sons John, William, Charles and Wright. **All four gave the same occupation: woollen weaver.** Their sister Ann was eighteen. [Baptism transcriptions](research/2026-11-04-charles-william-review.md) · [1851 census, HO 107/2291](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/6611c6f8f493fd652b692ca6/charles-bentley-1851-yorkshire-west-riding-almondbury-1828-).

That census catches the brothers together before their lives diverged. Charles raised his family in Marsden. Death registrations at St Kilda in Australia fit three of his brothers: William in 1891, Wright in 1902 and Joseph in 1907. Wright’s entry names his mother as Betty Bottomley. It is a small detail, but it ties an Australian record back to the woman at the head of the Carr Clough household half a century earlier. Their ships, departure dates and reasons for leaving remain unknown. [The St Kilda records](research/2026-11-05-australian-households.md) · [Carlisle Street newspaper notices](research/2026-11-10-binn-and-carlisle-street.md#1-three-bentley-households-on-carlisle-street-st-kilda).

## Three houses on Carlisle Street

Digitised Melbourne newspapers in the National Library of Australia’s Trove archive place all three St Kilda households on **Carlisle Street, St Kilda**. William Bentley’s death notice in *The Argus* on **17 October 1891**, page 1, and a first-anniversary notice in *The Leader* on **22 October 1892**, page 44, confirm that he died on **12 October 1891** at Carlisle Street West, St Kilda, in his sixty-sixth year, husband of Hannah Bentley. Hannah’s death notice on **21 December 1901**, page 9, records her death on **13 December 1901** at **62 Carlisle Street, St Kilda**, as William’s widow. Their eldest surviving son John William Bentley died at the same address, **62 Carlisle Street**, on **10 February 1950**, with his notice naming siblings Ann, Mary, James, Ada and Lisle. [William’s 1891 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/8486022) · [1892 In Memoriam](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/198822607) · [Hannah’s 1901 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/9621115) · [John William’s 1950 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/22756816).

Wright Bentley’s death notice in *The Age* on **28 July 1902**, page 1, confirms that he died on **27 July 1902** at his residence, **“Stockwell”, Carlisle Street, St Kilda**, in his seventy-first year, beloved husband of Helen Bentley. An *In Memoriam* notice in *The Argus* on **28 September 1932**, page 1, commemorates Helen Bentley, who died on **28 September 1931**, inserted by A. Bentley and Robert, Will and Raymond Hutchison—connecting Wright’s widow to the 1881 Victorian marriage registration of Wright Bentley (born Yorkshire) and Ellen Hutchinson. [Wright’s 1902 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/200927806) · [Helen’s 1932 In Memoriam](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/4500311).

Two doors from number 62, at **“Baroona”, 66 Carlisle Street, St Kilda**, **Ann Bentley**, widow of Joseph Bentley, died on **26 November 1915**, aged 75. Her death notice in *The Argus* on **27 November 1915**, page 13, names her children as **Arch., David, Mrs. R. O. Scott, Mrs. J. Armstrong, H. J. Bentley and Mrs. W. Bloomfield**. The Bright Cemetery Register (Entry 140) independently identifies Dorathy Jane Armstrong (1870–1954), born at St Kilda, as the daughter of Joseph Bentley and Ann Jones. Naming **Arch.** (Archibald, born 1859 at Point Nepean to Joseph Bentley and Irvine Nisbett) alongside the children of the 1865 marriage establishes that the Joseph who married Irvine Nisbett in 1854 and the Joseph who married Ann Jones in 1865 were the same man. A 2013 *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin* article mistakenly attributes the 1856 autobiography of the Saddleworth educational reformer Joseph Bentley (1805–1872) to a Joseph born at Stanedge in 1821; that memoir belongs to the 1805 school inspector, not to Betty’s son in St Kilda. [Ann’s 1915 notice](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/1591147) · [Bright Cemetery transcription](http://www.ozgenonline.com/~aust_cem_index/vic/alpine/bright/brightcemreg.htm) · [Full analysis and two-Joseph distinction](research/2026-11-10-binn-and-carlisle-street.md).

## Charles leaves weaving for stone

In 1861 Charles and Sarah Dyson were still at Carr Clough. Three-month-old John was at home with his sisters Elizabeth and Rebecca. Charles and his adult sister Ann both gave woollen weaving as their occupation. Ann’s presence matters: the family’s textile work can be followed through a woman as well as through her brothers. Below Carr Clough stood **Clough Lee Mills**, rebuilt in 1841 and expanded in 1860 by Robinson Bros, though the census does not name the employer of Charles or Ann. [1861 census, RG 9/3243](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906c0a3e9379091b1376b45/john-bentley-1861-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-) · [Clough Lee Mills history](https://www.marsdenhistory.co.uk/clough-lee-mills.php).

Ten years later Charles and Sarah were at Binn. Charles was now a **stone delver**, someone who dug or quarried stone. The enumerator listed fifteen-year-old Elizabeth as a bobbin winder and ten-year-old John as a woollen piecer, a job joining broken threads. In the space of two censuses, the father had changed both his home and his trade. Two of his children had gone into textiles. The record gives neither a quarry nor a mill name, but it tells us who was already working and how young they were. A Marsden History Group analysis of the **1881 census for Binn** counts **18 households and 78 residents**, naming both **Bentley** and **Haigh** among the resident families and recording 2 stone quarrymen, 2 stone masons, 4 weavers, 1 weaver and farmer, 1 piecer and 1 person living on their own means among the 34 employed inhabitants. [1871 census, RG 10/4340](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906ed93e9379091b1751b6a/john-bentley-1871-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-) · [1881 Binn census profile](https://www.marsdenhistory.co.uk/binn.php).

The proposal concerning the Wessenden reservoir road and Binn Lane appeared on 18 November 1870. At the foot of Binn Road, on the old **Ottiwells Mill** site beside Ottiwells Bridge, the Crowther family’s Marsden Mill Company invited tenders in 1876 for a new five-storey woollen mill that later became known as **Bank Bottom Mill**. Charles’s employer and quarry remain unknown. [Original notice, page 4969](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/23680/page/4969/data.pdf) · [Ottiwells and Bank Bottom Mill histories](research/2026-11-10-binn-and-carlisle-street.md#3-binn-in-1881-and-the-mills-below-binn-road).

## John the piecer becomes a weaver

Twenty years after the census counted John at work aged ten, another enumerator came to Binn. John was thirty, married to Sarah Ann Haigh, and employed as a woollen weaver. At home were Willie, six, Sarah H., four, Thomas Herbert, two, and Sam, one. There was also Dan Haigh, aged seventy-one, recorded as an uncle living on his own means. Three generations shared the address. Set the two censuses beside each other and the ten-year-old at work comes back into view as the father of four young children. [1891 census, RG 12/3554](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/590706c3e9379091b1941bc2/thomas-h-bentley-1891-yorkshire-west-riding-huddersfield-1889-).

Sarah Ann can also be found in the memorial index for **St Bartholomew’s, Marsden**. The inscription identifies her as John’s wife and as the daughter of Thomas and Harriet Haigh. It gives 10 August 1934 as her death date and her age as 77. An adjoining John Bentley entry gives 10 November 1918 and an age of 67. Our John’s census ages point to 57. The same memorial index block also lists **Sam Bentley** (husband of Rose Hannah Bentley, died 14 December 1966, age 82), **Rose Hannah Bentley** (died 13 November 1938, age 57) and their daughter **Ethel Bentley** (died 29 April 1915, age 6), alongside Lesley Cawthorn Bentley (died 1911, age 2) and Irving Edward and Edith Ivy Bentley. Sam is a candidate for John and Sarah Ann’s youngest son in 1891 (born c.1890), though the transcribed age of 82 in 1966 presents a date discrepancy similar to John’s entry. [Marsden History Group memorial index, printed page 7](https://www.marsdenhistory.co.uk/downloads/graveyard_index.pdf#page=8) · [Full Bentley memorial table](research/2026-11-10-binn-and-carlisle-street.md#4-six-further-bentley-memorials-at-st-bartholomews-marsden).

Two years before the 1891 census, Dyson and Co. advertised a change in its partnership at **Upper Bank Bottom Mill**, Marsden (“Th’ Top Bonk”), while Huddersfield Corporation proposed **Butterley Reservoir** in the same month. Local historical accounts from the Marsden History Group and Kirklees Council show that **Huddersfield Corporation demolished Upper Bank Bottom Mill in 1891** to build Butterley Reservoir, and its site now lies buried under the far end of the reservoir embankment. **Lower Bank Bottom Mill** (rented by John Crowther and Sons from 1867/1870 until 1901, and demolished in 1913 as a reservoir storehouse) and the Crowthers’ five-storey mill on the **Ottiwells** site at the foot of Binn Road continued operating. John’s specific mill is still unknown. [Original notice, 29 November 1889, page 6867](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/25997/page/6867/data.pdf) · [Upper Bank Bottom Mill history](https://www.marsdenhistory.co.uk/upper-bank-bottom-mill.php) · [Kirklees Swellands walking guide, PDF](https://www.kirklees.gov.uk/beta/countryside/pdf/swelands-walking-route.pdf).

## Thomas Herbert and Lauretta in the local paper

Thomas Herbert was the two-year-old at Binn in 1891. His marriage to Lauretta Jones was registered in Huddersfield in the first quarter of 1920. Forty-three years later the couple appear together again, this time in the *Colne Valley Guardian*. A death notice published on **27 September 1963**, page 8, names Lauretta as Thomas Herbert’s wife and reports that she died on **25 September**, aged 75. That named relationship helps distinguish her from people with similar names and gives September precedence over a July date passed down in the family. The available newspaper extract is machine-read. The address is too garbled to publish with confidence. [Newspaper reference and evidence](research/2026-11-04-wider-family.md) · [Marriage and census references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

Thomas Herbert was about twenty-five when the First World War began. I could not verify a service number, regiment or military record linked to his Marsden household. A newspaper search illustrates the risk of filling in that gap from a name alone. An apparent *London Gazette* hit for his full name leads to a 1905 page that lists a Herbert Bentley of Bingley among temporary assistant postmen and a separate Thomas Herbert elsewhere on the page. The printed notice gives us no job or service history for Lauretta’s husband. [Original Gazette page, 1 August 1905](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/27823/page/5301/data.pdf).

## Roy and Margaret in postwar Yorkshire

Roy brings the story back within family memory. His birth was registered at Huddersfield in spring 1930, with Jones given as his mother’s maiden name. His father Thomas Herbert had spent his early childhood at Binn. Roy married Margaret Cartwright in the autumn of 1953, in the Upper Agbrigg registration district. The two marriage entries point to each other through their surnames and share the same reference, volume 2d, page 989. That gives their marriage a firmer date than we currently have for Roy’s work or any National Service. The family reports that Roy later married Betty and Margaret also remarried. Margaret died before the reporting relative was born. That relative was six when Roy died at Fartown in 1998. The 1991 death entry under Bentley and the exact birth date taken from it remain unconfirmed. [Roy and Margaret’s civil registration references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

## The road below the reservoir

Family recollection supplied 9 November 2026. Thomas Herbert’s great-grandchild identifies his place of death as a terrace on Binn Road below the reservoir, recalls the family move with Roy to a Slaithwaite farm, and suggests Roy carried it on and Thomas moved back. The precise holding, tenure and dates are open. The same contributor remembers visiting a maternal relative further up Binn Lane as a child in the 1990s and walking around Butterley and Wessenden for years before learning the Bentley connection. The two houses are distinct in this account. The published story keeps the relative and house unidentified.

The census transcriptions say Binn in 1871 and 1891. They establish three generations in the locality across the two returns. Thomas’s death registration establishes the district and quarter. The terrace detail comes from the family. [1871 household](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/5906ed93e9379091b1751b6a/john-bentley-1871-yorkshire-west-riding-slaithwaite-1861-) · [1891 household](https://www.freecen.org.uk/search_records/590706c3e9379091b1941bc2/thomas-h-bentley-1891-yorkshire-west-riding-huddersfield-1889-) · [Civil registration references](research/2026-10-10-line-from-records.md).

The contemporary Ordnance Survey sheet Yorkshire CCLIX.11 was surveyed in 1890 and published in 1892. The lower part of the sheet labels Binn Road, Lane Syke, a quarry, Bank Bottom Bridge and Bank Bottom Mill (woollen). Several groups of houses are individually named. These are map labels, not evidence that a particular Bentley occupied a particular house or worked in the quarry. [Ordnance Survey sheet, National Library of Scotland](https://maps.nls.uk/view/125647278).

The [18 November 1870 notice, p. 4969](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/23680/page/4969/data.pdf) names Binn Lane as an endpoint of the road from Wessenden Reservoir and proposes altering, widening and diverting that road. This establishes the name by 1870. It gives neither the date Binn Lane was first laid out nor the route of every modern stretch. A Huddersfield Waterworks notice of 22 November 1889 proposed Butterley Reservoir and referred to the existing road leading from Binn Lane to Wessenden Reservoir. Local histories from the Marsden History Group and Kirklees Council show that **Upper Bank Bottom Mill** (“Th’ Top Bonk”) was demolished by Huddersfield Corporation in 1891 during the construction of Butterley Reservoir—its site now lying beneath the far end of the embankment—and **Lower Bank Bottom Mill** was demolished in 1913 for use as a reservoir storehouse, while the Crowthers’ five-storey mill on the **Ottiwells** site at the foot of Binn Road expanded into the modern Bank Bottom Mill. A Huddersfield Corporation notice of 22 November 1901 described Butterley Reservoir as under construction. In January 1964 a Colne Valley council speed-limit proposal used the junction of Mount Road, Carrs Road, Fall Lane and Binn Road as a reference point. This independently places the road name in use shortly before Thomas’s death. None of the notices names Thomas or gives a house number. The free searches in this pass have not identified the Slaithwaite farm, though the Marsden History Group’s 1900–50 farming notes record a Bentley woman making butter to supplement wartime rations at **Binn Lodge Farm** (her first name is withheld here until a death date is verified). Later West Riding census searches for Thomas Bentley, born 1887–90, produced no match in the FreeCEN transcriptions queried. Coverage and name variants limit those searches. Neither the negative results nor the map establish his movements.

## Where the military and newspaper searches led

The Marsden Park memorial covers both world wars and St Bartholomew’s has a First World War parishioners’ memorial. The published transcription of the parish tablet lists the fallen. No Bentley appears in that transcription. Thomas Herbert survived the war, so this tablet cannot settle whether he served. I could not verify a named Bentley in our households from those listings. The Imperial War Museums service search and National Archives catalogue returned no usable individual record in this search. Service remains an open question, including for men who survived the war. [Marsden Park memorial](https://www.warmemorialsonline.org.uk/memorial/138580/) · [Parish memorial](https://www.warmemorialsonline.org.uk/memorial/156214/).

Wright is an unusual first name, but even here a newspaper hit needs care. An 1849 notice names **Wright Bentley** in an Oldham iron and brass business with John Bentley and James Booth. Later notices record an Oldham ironfounder’s partnership dissolution and a bankruptcy petition. Our Wright appears as a woollen weaver with Betty at Carr Clough in 1851, and died on 27 July 1902 at “Stockwell”, Carlisle Street, St Kilda. I found no address, relative or other identifying detail joining him to the ironfounder. Those business events therefore belong to the Oldham man’s history until his identity can be established.
