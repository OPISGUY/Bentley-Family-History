## 1. The family connection

The research centres on the Bentley family associated with High Bentley Hall, Shelf, near
Halifax, West Yorkshire, England. You live near High Bentley Hall and have a personal connection
to the family history. You have also mentioned that family wealth continued into later
generations, until your grandfather sold his plot of land in Slaithwaite, Marsden, Saddleworth.

The main genealogical question is whether your Bentley ancestors had been established in West
Yorkshire for centuries, or whether an earlier branch came from Monmouth, Monmouthshire, Wales.

## 3. The Yorkshire evidence

Several historical leads suggest that the Bentley surname was established in the Shelf and
Halifax area long before John G. Bentley's recorded birth.

### High Bentley Hall, Shelf

The historic property has late-medieval timber-framed fabric, dating broadly to the second half
of the 15th century. This establishes the age of parts of the building, but does not establish
who owned or occupied it at that time.

[Historic England listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1133765)

### Bentley family references, 1296 and 1379

The local-history resource Calderdale Companion records a Bentley family in Shelf in 1296 and a
Thomas de Bentley in 1379. These are promising leads for investigating the family's earlier
presence in Yorkshire. They do not yet establish a direct ancestral link to John G.

[Calderdale Companion](https://www.calderdalecompanion.co.uk/)

### Earlier Bentley references

The same local-history research points to Bentley surname references dating back to the early
13th century. These could help establish how long the name was associated with the region, but
each individual and family relationship needs to be verified against the underlying records.

## 4. The Monmouth question

The online pedigree proposes that Sir John Bentley lived in Monmouth and was born around 1470,
dying in 1527.

At present, we have not established a contemporary record proving that Sir John was the father of
John G. Bentley. Nor have we found evidence demonstrating that the Yorkshire Bentleys originated
in Monmouth.

There are therefore two main possibilities:

- **Long-established Yorkshire family:** John G. Bentley belonged to a Bentley family already
  established around Shelf and Halifax, potentially for generations before his birth.
- **Earlier migration from Wales:** an earlier generation of the family moved from Monmouth or
  elsewhere in Wales to Yorkshire.

A third possibility is that the Monmouth pedigree has been incorrectly attached to the Yorkshire
family tree.

We should keep all three possibilities open until records distinguish between them.

## 6. Current assessment

The most promising direction is to investigate the Yorkshire Bentley family independently, rather
than assuming the Monmouth connection is correct.

The early local references and the medieval fabric of High Bentley Hall support further
investigation of a longstanding Yorkshire presence. They do not yet prove that John G. descended
from the people recorded in 1296 or 1379.

The next practical step is to inspect the actual source citations attached to John G. Bentley's
Ancestry profile, especially the source for his 1504 birthplace and the source naming his
parents. That may reveal whether the Monmouth connection comes from a published pedigree or has
simply been copied from another online tree.

**Research goal:** establish a documented line of descent from John G. Bentley of High Bentley
Hall back through earlier Yorkshire generations, and only then determine whether there is
credible evidence connecting the family to Monmouth or an earlier location.
