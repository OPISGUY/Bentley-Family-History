# Who lived at Binn in 1801?

The year of the sale of “Bentleys” on the Saddleworth side gives us a date to test against records on the Marsden side. The family can be placed at Carr Clough in 1851 and at Binn in 1871. Earlier evidence now identifies several people who held land or occupied cottages at Binn, including people named Haigh and Dyson, both surnames that later appear in the Bentley family.

## John Haigh’s looms, 1697

The Yorkshire Historical Dictionary quotes John Haigh of Binn’s 1697 will in its entry for the word “stock”. The extract lists wool cards and “paire of Lomes”. Its source catalogue identifies the holding as Peculiar Probate, Marsden, Borthwick Institute for Archives. The published extract establishes a named household with textile equipment. The complete will would be needed to see which relatives, if any, it names. [Dictionary entry](https://yorkshiredictionary.york.ac.uk/words/stock) · [Archive identification](https://yorkshiredictionary.york.ac.uk/source_materials/2288).

## Landholders in the year of the sale

Marsden History Group transcribes a list of farm occupiers for 1801. An asterisk identifies a person who also owned the holding. Five entries give the address simply as Binn.

| Recorded holding | Occupiers | Recorded as owners |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binn | Luke Campenott, Mary Carter, John Fielding, Thomas Haigh, John Whitehead | John Fielding, Thomas Haigh, John Whitehead |
| Binn House | William Firth | William Firth |
| Binn Side | Silvanus Shaw | Ownership unspecified |

Matthew Flint appears at Carr Clough. No Bentley appears among the named occupiers in the published list. Its scope leaves open the possibility of a lodger, a cottage tenant or someone in another person's household. The evidence for William and Betty’s children still places them at Standage in the early nineteenth century. A move direct from the sale to Binn remains unverified. ## The cottages behind one name

On 15 May 1822 John Fielding of Binn made his will. His own house went to his grandson John. A second dwelling, occupied by James Shaw, went to Fielding’s daughter Hannah Firth for her lifetime. Another cottage, occupied by James Dyson, went to his daughter Mary Marsden for hers. The inventory is dated 23 October 1828 and probate 20 April 1829. The archive reference uses the spelling Fielden. These documents show why a farm list can miss people who actually lived in the hamlet. James Dyson’s relationship, if any, to Charles Bentley’s wife Sarah Dyson remains unknown. [Will and inventory transcription](https://probate.southpenninehistorygroup.org.uk/probate-records/%2834%29%20John%20Fielding%20of%20Binn%20Yman%20Will%201822%20Inv%201828%20ACB.pdf).

## Where the Haigh evidence leads

Sarah Ann Bentley’s Marsden memorial names her parents as Thomas and Harriet Haigh. Another inscription gives a Thomas Haigh, husband of Harriet, aged 60 in 1881. If these were Sarah Ann’s parents, that Thomas was born around 1821, a generation too late to have owned land in 1801. Establishing his parents through a marriage or baptism, and looking for heirs in the earlier Thomas’s property records, would test a connection. For now the earlier Haigh entries establish the surname at Binn across two centuries.
