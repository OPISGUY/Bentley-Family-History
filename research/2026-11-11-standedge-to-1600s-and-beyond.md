# Standedge to the 1600s and beyond: probate, map, parish and manor evidence

_11 November 2026_

This pass targets the four hardest open questions on the Saddleworth and Pennine line from the early nineteenth century back to the seventeenth century:

1. **Can we close or narrow the gap between Jonas Bentley (d. 1686), Mathew ("old Matthew", 1664–1735/6) and Matthew Bentley of Standedge (d. 1751)?**
2. **What connects the 1788 William Bently (son of John and Ann of Standedge) to the William Bentley of Standage who married Betty Bottomley in 1815, and where exactly was the family's freehold tenement called "Bentleys"?**
3. **What new primary or published evidence exists for Matthew and Sarah Bentley's household (1722–1751) and their daughter Helen (Ellin) Platt (1733/4–1769)?**
4. **Why do no Bentleys appear in the 1641/2 Saddleworth Protestation Return or the 1664–1674 Hearth Tax, how do the Diggle and Boarshurst Bentleys fit into the seventeenth- and eighteenth-century parish network, and what comparators exist across the West Riding and Lancashire border before 1613?**

All research in this note uses **free, publicly accessible sources without registration or payment**. Every claim is assigned an explicit status (**Documented**, **Probable**, **Supported** or **Not yet proven**) in accordance with [family-tree.md](../family-tree.md).

## 1. Jonas Bentley at Standedge in 1686 and the four-point correlation to Matthew of Standedge (d. 1751)

### 1.1 Jonas Bentley's 1686 inventory places him at Standedge forty years earlier than the parish registers

Until now, the earliest record in this repository naming **Standedge** (*Stone-edge*) as the family's home was the 10 April 1726 baptism of **Sarah**, daughter of Matthew and Sarah Bentley of *Stone-edge*, followed by the 26 February 1735/6 burial of **Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge ("old Matthew")**. Neither the St Chad's parish register entries for Jonas Bentley (baptisms of son **John** on 27 August 1654 and son **Mathew** on 11 December 1664; burial of **Jonah Bentley** on 6 July 1686) nor the printed Record Society of Lancashire and Cheshire index to Chester probate records (*"Bentley, Jonas, of Saddleworth, Admon with Inv 1686"*) named the hamlet within Saddleworth where Jonas lived.

An inspection of the **Leonard Kidd Genealogical Collection (`H/KID`)** in the [Saddleworth Historical Society Archives Catalogue (Rev. 4, p. 248)](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/SHS-Archives-Catalogue-Rev-4.pdf#page=248) resolves Jonas's residence directly from the probate papers:

- **Catalogue entry (`H/KID`, Will and Probate Material, p. 248):**
  > `BENTLEY, Jonas, of Standedge, Saddleworth; Admon 1686; Inventory 18 August 1686.`

Immediately beside it in the same collection (`H/KID`, p. 248) is the abstract of the younger Matthew's will:

- **Catalogue entry (`H/KID`, Will and Probate Material, p. 248):**
  > `BENTLEY, Matthew, Clothier of Standedge, Saddleworth; Will dated 18 November 1751; Probate 3 March 1753.`

**Why this matters:**
- **Documented (via archive catalogue abstract of the original 18 August 1686 inventory):** **Jonas Bentley** was already living **"of Standedge, Saddleworth"** at the time of his death in July–August 1686.
- This pushes the Bentley presence at **Standedge** back **forty years** earlier than previously documented (from 1726 back to **1686**), placing **Jonas Bentley (d. 1686)**, **"old Matthew" Bentley of Stone-edge (bur. 26 February 1735/6)** and **Matthew Bentley of Standedge, clothier (bur. 20 November 1751)** at the **same Pennine locality across three consecutive adult generations**.

### 1.2 The four-point residence and naming correlation between "old Matthew" (1664–1735/6) and Matthew of Standedge (d. 1751)

No surviving baptism entry at St Chad's between 1685 and 1705 names a son **Matthew** born to Mathew Bentley—a period when the printed St Chad's registers have well-known gaps (most notably in the 1690s, where John Radcliffe had to reconstruct missing years from fragmentary transcripts). Because no single parish or probate transcript currently in hand names Matthew (d. 1751) as the son of "old Matthew" (bur. 1735/6), the link remains **Probable** rather than **Documented**. However, combining the newly established 1686 Standedge abode with the parish registers produces a four-part documentary correlation:

| # | Evidence point | Earlier generation (Jonas d. 1686 & Mathew / Ellin) | Younger generation (Matthew of Standedge d. 1751 & Sarah) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Continuous Standedge residence & contemporary byname** | **Jonas Bentley** is **of Standedge** in his 18 August 1686 inventory (`H/KID`, p. 248). **Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge** is buried at St Chad's on **26 February 1735/6** as **"old Matthew"**. | **Matthew Bentley of Standedge, clothier**, is already a married householder at **Stone-edge / Standedge** from at least **10 April 1726** until his burial on **20 November 1751**—explaining why the parish clerk in February 1735/6 had to distinguish the elder resident of the same hamlet as **"old Matthew"**. |
| 2 | **The name Ellin / Helen** | Mathew Bentley's wife **Ellin** (*Elline*, wife of Mathew Bentley) was buried at St Chad's on **5 May 1719**. | Matthew and Sarah named a daughter **Helen** (baptised **20 January 1733/4** at Knar; written **Ellen** in her 1769 child's baptism and **Ellin** on her 1769 gravestone at St Chad's). |
| 3 | **The sibling pair Ester (1700) and John (1702/3)** | The only two named children recorded at St Chad's for Mathew Bentley in 1700–1703 are **Ester**, daughter of Mathew (**bap. 15 September 1700**), and **John**, son of Mathew (**bap. 14 February 1702/3**; an unnamed daughter was buried 26 December 1702). | Matthew and Sarah named a son **John** (**bap. 23 February 1728/9** at Stone-edge) and their next daughter **Esther** (**bap. 7 November 1731** at Stone-edge), repeating both names from the 1700–1703 household. |
| 4 | **Timing of the name Matthew (1738/9)** | **"Old Matthew" of Stone-edge** lived until **February 1735/6**. During old Matthew's lifetime, Matthew and Sarah baptised sons **James** (1722) and **John** (1728/9), but did not name a son Matthew. | The very first son born to Matthew and Sarah after old Matthew's burial in February 1735/6 was baptised **Matthew** on **21 January 1738/9** at Stone-edge. |

## 2. Matthew and Sarah Bentley at Deanhead, Knowl and Standedge, and Helen (Ellin) Platt's 1769 gravestone

### 2.1 Clara Shaw's Knowl gazetteer (*SHS Bulletin* 12(1), Spring 1982)

In Matthew and Sarah's baptism series, their first two children—**James** (25 March 1722) and **Mary** (5 January 1723/4)—record the family at **Dean-head** and **Knowll**, before **Sarah** (10 April 1726) and subsequent entries record **Stone-edge** (and **Knar** in 1733/4).

Clara Shaw's historical gazetteer of **Knowl** in the *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, Vol. 12, No. 1 (Spring 1982), pp. 13–15 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol12no1-2.pdf#page=15)), traces the inhabitants of Knowl (Knowle Farm, on the western slope of Standedge above Knarr Barn) from the parish registers and deeds:

- **Page 14:** Explicitly notes: *"Prior to 1724, Matthew and Sarah Bentley lived at Deanhead"* before moving to **Knowl** (where Mary was baptised in January 1723/4) and **Standedge**.
- **Page 14 (Unplaced Knowl contemporary):** The same gazetteer records that **Martha Bentley, spinster of Knowl**, had a daughter **Mary** christened at St Chad's on **14 July 1744**. Martha is not among the baptised children of Matthew and Sarah (whose youngest daughter Betty was baptised on 26 February 1743/4), and is recorded here as an unplaced contemporary in the same hamlet.

### 2.2 Abraham Bentley (c.1708–1792) at Acre (below Deanhead) in 1750 and Standedge in 1792

In [research/2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md](2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md#5-old-matthew-16641736-jonas-died-1686-and-mathew-fl-1613), **Abraham Bentley of Standedge**, buried at St Chad's on **23 February 1792, aged 84** (born c.1708, a year when St Chad's baptisms are sparse), was noted as a probable brother or close kinsman of Matthew Bentley of Standedge (d. 1751).

The historical gazetteer for **Ackers / Acre / Acre Barn** in the *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, Vol. 13, No. 1 (Spring 1983), p. 15 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol13no1.pdf#page=17); see also Vol. 4, No. 2, p. 21), provides an earlier residence for Abraham:

- **Acre (Grid Ref. SE 006093, on the hillside immediately below the Horse and Jockey Inn at Deanhead):**
  > In **1750**, **Abraham Bentley, clothier**, lived at **Acre**.

This places **Abraham Bentley, clothier**, immediately below **Deanhead** in 1750 before his burial from **Standedge** in 1792 aged 84—mirroring Matthew and Sarah's own movement between **Deanhead** (1722) and **Standedge** (1726–1751). Moreover, when **Mary Bentley of Standedge** (probable daughter of James Bentley and Mary Nield, bap. 1 April 1756) married **John Hall** in 1781, they named two of their sons at Standedge **Abraham Hall** (bap. 24 October 1788) and **Jonas Hall** (bap. 16 April 1797)—preserving both **Abraham** and the seventeenth-century ancestral name **Jonas** in the Standedge family.

### 2.3 Helen (Ellin) Platt née Bentley's death (1 September 1769), burial (9 September 1769) and gravestone at St Chad's

In [family-groups.md](../family-groups.md#9-matthew-bentley-of-standedge-and-sarah) and [family-groups.md](../family-groups.md#10-helen-bentley-and-thomas-platt), **Helen Bentley** (bap. **20 January 1733/4** at Knar, daughter of Matthew and Sarah) was connected to **Thomas Platt** through L. N. Kidd's article *"Some descendants of the Platt family of Thorns, Saddleworth"*, *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, Vol. 24, No. 3 (Autumn 1994), pp. 1–7 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol24no3-2.pdf#page=6)).

Checking pages 3–4 of Kidd's article supplies her exact death date, burial date, age at death and gravestone inscription at St Chad's, which now directly corroborate her 20 January 1733/4 baptism:

- **Death and burial (Kidd 1994, p. 4):** Following the baptism of her youngest child **Joseph Platt** on **24 August 1769** (when the family lived at Heyhouse), **Helen Platt died on 1 September 1769, in her 36th year**, and was **buried at St Chad's, Saddleworth, on 9 September 1769**. The baby **Joseph Platt** was buried at St Chad's on **17 November 1769**.
- **Gravestone at St Chad's (Kidd 1994, p. 4):** Her husband **Thomas Platt** died twenty years later on **9 September 1789** and was buried at St Chad's on **12 September 1789**. Their shared gravestone in the old churchyard at Saddleworth Church describes Thomas as of **Whams Inn** and inscribes her name as **Ellin**—the exact spelling used for **"old Matthew"'s wife Ellin** (buried 5 May 1719).
- **Age corroboration:** Dying on **1 September 1769 "in her 36th year"** means she had turned 35 and had not yet reached her 36th birthday, placing her birth between **2 September 1733 and 1 September 1734**—an exact match for the **20 January 1733/4** (1734 new style) baptism of **Helen, daughter of Matthew and Sarah Bentley, of Knar**.

## 3. Resolving the "Bentleys" tenement (1791–1822) and the 6 June 1819 double baptism at St Chad's

### 3.1 Where was "Bentleys"? The 1791–1806 deeds (`D-AAB/1/1–1/5`) and the 1822 Map of Saddleworth

Earlier research plans ([earlier-research-plan.md](../earlier-research-plan.md#5-earlier-saddleworth-ancestry)) asked two linked topographic questions:
1. Investigate the **"Bentleys" deeds** at Oldham Local Studies and Archives (`D-AAB/1/1–1/5`, 1791–1806) through public catalogues or transcriptions.
2. Establish whether the mid-eighteenth-century Grade II listed **Bentley Farmhouse at Bleak Hey Nook** (Historic England List Entry 1067452, Whimberry Lee Lane) is the property called **"Bentleys"** in those deeds.

Cross-referencing the Oldham Archives catalogue entries (`D-AAB/1/1–1/5`), a verbatim abstract of the **25–26 November 1791** lease and release published on [`walterfitzgilbertdehamilton.com`](http://www.walterfitzgilbertdehamilton.com/2017/04/barnburgh-cadeby-high-melton-and.html), the **Geo. C. Hirst Deed Box 2 (`H/GCH`)** abstracts in the [SHS Archives Catalogue (Rev. 4, pp. 235–236)](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/SHS-Archives-Catalogue-Rev-4.pdf#page=235), and Eric Watson's article *"The Stanedge Turnpikes"* in the *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, Vol. 29, No. 1 (Spring 1999), pp. 6–10 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Vol-29-No-1-Spring-1999.pdf#page=8)), resolves the geography cleanly:

1. **The two Bentley clothiers already lived at two distinct places in November 1791:**
   - In `D-AAB/1/1` (**5 November 1791**) and `D-AAB/1/2` (**25–26 November 1791**), when William Farrer Esq. (lord of the manor of Saddleworth) and his trustees conveyed the freehold of the tenement called **"Bentleys"** with 8 acres of land and common of pasture, the two joint purchasers are described as:
     - **James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothier**, and
     - **John Bentley of Bentleys, clothier**.
   - In `D-AAB/1/5` (**1–2 May 1806**), when the estate was sold to **Joseph Scholfield of Blakehey Nook, drysalter**, the vendors are again **James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothier**, and **John Bentley of Standedge, clothier** (together with their mortgagee John Whitehead of Quick Edge).
2. **"Bentleys" is mapped separately from Bleak Hey Nook on the 1822 Map of Saddleworth:**
   - In Eric Watson's *"The Stanedge Turnpikes"* (*SHS Bulletin* 29(1), Spring 1999, p. 8), a reproduced section of the **1822 Map of Saddleworth** (surveyed before the 1826 enclosure awards and also published in *Mapping Saddleworth Vol. II: Manuscript Maps of the Parish, 1625–1822*, 2010) explicitly marks **"BENTLEYS"** and **"STANEDGE FOOT"** at the foot of the Standedge escarpment near **Burk Clough**, **Millstone Edge** and **Thieves Bridge**, higher up the turnpike road toward the Standedge cutting than **Bleak Hey Nook**.
   - Later deeds in the same Oldham archive bundle (`D-AAB/1/6–1/12`, 1859–1880) describe the same holding as *"a tenement known as Bentleys, comprising two cottages at Rough, near Standedge"* and add *"a newly built house at Standedge Foot"* in 1861. (An earlier historical note in *SHS Bulletin* 1(3), September 1971, p. 3, [PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol1no3.pdf#page=3), records that *Standedge Foot* was originally built in 1680 and served for a time as a wayside inn where coach drivers watered their horses before tackling Standedge Hill.)
   - Meanwhile, the Grade II listed **Bentley Farmhouse** on Whimberry Lee Lane at **Bleak Hey Nook** matches the residence of **James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothier** (the co-owner in 1791–1806), rather than the higher Standedge Foot / Rough tenement called **"Bentleys"** where **John Bentley** lived.
3. **Township division (Lordsmere):**
   - In the SHS Archives Catalogue (`H/GCH`, Box 2, Items 44 & 46, p. 235), a 1792 lease assignment of *The Upper Rough near Bleakhey Nook* to James Schofield of Blakehey Nook is paired with an **1843 Declaration of Merger of Tithes** for land at **"Knowl Brow, Lords Mere, Saddleworth"** owned by **Joseph Schofield of Blakehey Nook** (the 1806 purchaser of "Bentleys"). This confirms that the Bleak Hey Nook / Knowl Brow / Standedge Foot holdings lay within the **Lords Mere (Lordsmere)** division of Saddleworth.

### 3.2 The 6 June 1819 double baptism at St Chad's: connecting William (bap. 1788) and James (bap. 1784) at Standage

The central open question in the nineteenth-century pedigree is whether **William Bentley of Standage, clothier**—who married **Betty Bottomley** at St Chad's on **5 January 1815** and baptised our ancestor **Charles Bentley** on **8 June 1828**—is the **William Bently** baptised at St Chad's on **8 April 1788** to **John Bentley (1750–1789)** and **Ann Broadbent** of Standedge / Meerleech.

As noted in [family-groups.md](../family-groups.md#7-john-bentley-and-ann-broadbent), John Bentley (1750–1789) and Ann Broadbent left **three surviving sons** at Standedge:
1. **John Bentley**, baptised **27 October 1781** (Stannage)
2. **James Bentley**, baptised **24 October 1784** (Standedge)
3. **William Bently**, baptised **8 April 1788** (Meerleech)

In the [Saddleworth Historical Society St Chad's baptism database](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/baptisms-search-results/?surname=Bentley) (inspected previously in [research/2026-11-05-standedge-households.md](2026-11-05-standedge-households.md)), two Standage clothier households—**William and Betty Bentley** and **James and Ann Bentley**—appear side by side throughout the 1810s and 1820s. A date-by-date comparison of their baptisms reveals a striking synchrony on **Sunday, 6 June 1819**, together with the shared introduction of the given name **Charles**:

| Date of baptism (St Chad's) | Child | Parents | Abode | Occupation | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **6 June 1819** | **William BENTLEY** | **William and Betty BENTLEY** | **Standage** | **Clothier** | Baptised at St Chad's on the **exact same day** as James and Ann's son Charles below. |
| **6 June 1819** | **Charles BENTLEY** | **James and Ann BENTLEY** | **Standage** | **Clothier** | Baptised at St Chad's on the **exact same day** as William and Betty's son William above. First appearance of the forename **Charles** in the Standedge Bentley families. |
| **8 June 1828** | **Charles BENTLEY** | **William and Betty BENTLEY** | **Standage** | **Clothier** | William and Betty also give a son the forename **Charles**—the direct ancestor in Group 5. |

**Why this matters:**
- **Documented (via SHS St Chad's baptism transcription):** On **6 June 1819**, **William Bentley of Standage, clothier** (with wife Betty) and **James Bentley of Standage, clothier** (with wife Ann, née **Annaminny/Annamina Broadbent**, married at St Chad's 28 August 1808) stood at St Chad's on the **same day** to baptise their sons **William** and **Charles**.
- Neither **Charles** nor **Wright** had appeared in earlier generations of the Standedge Bentleys, yet **James and Ann of Standage** named a son **Charles** on **6 June 1819** (the day of the joint baptism with William and Betty), and **William and Betty of Standage** subsequently named our ancestor **Charles** on **8 June 1828**.
- Taken together with their shared residence (**Standage**), shared occupation (**Clothier**), and the exact match of their forenames (**James** and **William**) to the two younger surviving sons of **John Bentley (1750–1789) and Ann Broadbent** (**James**, bap. 1784, and **William**, bap. 1788), this joint baptism provides strong circumstantial corroboration (**Probable**) that **William (husband of Betty)** and **James (husband of Ann)** were brothers at Standedge.
- In accordance with our evidence rules, because neither the 1815 marriage entry nor the 1815–1831 baptism entries explicitly name William's father, the identification of Betty's husband with the 1788 William remains **Probable** rather than **Documented**.

### 3.3 Matthew Bentley and Thomas Rhodes at Woodmans (1784–1791), plus Coat Hill and Ridge

An open question in [earlier-research-plan.md](../earlier-research-plan.md#follow-up-to-the-5-november-search) was to *"Identify Matthew Bentley at Woodmans in 1784; he is not the man who died in 1751."*

In the *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, **Vol. 22, No. 2 (Summer 1992), p. 22** ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/vol22no2-2.pdf#page=24)), a verbatim transcription of a *Manchester Mercury* real-estate auction notice dated **19 April 1791**—advertising an auction to be held on **Friday, 26 November 1791** (the exact same day as the release `D-AAB/1/2` by which James Bentley of Blakehey Nook and John Bentley of Bentleys bought the freehold of **"Bentleys"**)—describes the two lots of the tenement called **Woodmans**, situated in Saddleworth near Delph:

- **Lot 1st (*SHS Bulletin* 22(2), p. 22):**
  > A shippon or edifice called the **Park-Coat**, and closes of land called the **Park Meadow, the Boot Meadow, the Deep Clough, the Ridding, the Lower One Furrow, and the One Furrow Top**, containing by admeasurement **9 Acres 3 Roods and 33 Perches** of statute measure, *"being part of a Tenement called WOODMANS, situated in Saddleworth, and near to Delph aforesaid, and now in the several possessions of the said William Bell, and of **Matthew Bentley**, and **Thomas Rhodes**, as Under-tenants to Charles Scanlon."*
- **Lot 2nd (*SHS Bulletin* 22(2), p. 22):**
  > *"Consisting of all that new erected Messuage or Dwelling-House, called WOODMANS aforesaid (lately converted into and now occupied as two Dwellings), and the Shippon thereto adjoining; also an ancient Cottage, or other Edifice standing near thereto, and the several Closes or Parcels of Land, commonly called or known by the several Names of the Limed, the Croft, the Bank, the Woodman's Meadow, and the Little One Furrow, containing by Admeasurement **9 Acres 3 Roods and 12 Perches** of Land, of Statute Measure or thereabouts, be the same more or less, and now in the several Possessions of the said **Matthew Bentley** and **Thomas Rhodes** as Under-tenants to the said Charles Scanlon."*

**Why this co-tenancy matters:**
- **Documented co-tenancy (1791):** **Matthew Bentley** and **Thomas Rhodes** jointly occupied the two dwellings and closes of **Woodmans** as under-tenants to Charles Scanlon in April 1791 (following Matthew's earlier 1784 recorded presence at Woodmans).
- **Kinship context (`Supported`):** In the Standedge family ([Group 9](../family-groups.md#9-matthew-bentley-of-standedge-and-sarah)), **Matthew Bentley** (baptised **21 January 1738/9** at Knoll, younger son of Matthew Bentley of Standedge, d. 1751, and Sarah) was the uncle of **Esther Bentley, "spinster of Standedge"** (baptised **14 April 1753** at Stone-edge, daughter of James Bentley and Mary Nield in [Group 8](../family-groups.md#8-james-bentley-and-mary-nield)). On **4 April 1779**, Esther Bentley had a son **William** baptised at St Chad's whose father was named in the register as **Thomas Rhodes of Warrockhill** ([research/2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md](2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md#1-william-bentley-baptised-1788)). Finding **Matthew Bentley** and **Thomas Rhodes** sharing the two-dwelling farmhouse at **Woodmans** in 1791 strongly supports identifying the Woodmans tenant (1784–1791) with **Matthew Bentley (bap. 21 January 1738/9)**, living alongside the father of his niece Esther's child.
- **Coat Hill and Ridge context:** In [Group 6](../family-groups.md#6-william-bentley-and-betty-probably-betty-bottomley), William and Betty's youngest sons were baptised at **Coat Hill** (Wright, 9 January 1831) and **Ridge** (Harry, 29 November 1835, status "Widow"). *SHS Bulletin* Vol. 29, No. 3, p. 1 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Vol-29-No-3-Autumn-1999.pdf#page=3), transcribing Becket Whitehead's notebook and the 1715 will of John Whitehead of Lydgate, stapler) documents the close called **Coat Hill** alongside Quickedge and Lane-end, while **Ridge** was enclosed and farmed alongside the **Bottomleys of Ridge**.

## 4. The seventeenth-century evidence boundary and the wider Saddleworth Bentley network

### 4.1 Why no Bentley appears in the 1641/2 Protestation Return or the 1664–1674 Hearth Tax

To test whether a continuous male-line Bentley household can be traced in Saddleworth between **2 December 1614** (baptism of Mathew, son of Mathew Bentley) and **16 August 1654** (birth of John, son of Jonas Bentley), two comprehensive seventeenth-century nominal tax and allegiance lists for Saddleworth were checked:

1. **The 1641/2 Protestation Return for Saddleworth (February 1641/2):**
   - Published in full in John Radcliffe (ed.), *The Parish Registers of St. Chad, Saddleworth, in the County of York, from 1613 to 1751* (Uppermill, 1887), Appendix, pp. 483–487 ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/parishregisters00radcgoog)).
   - The return lists adult males aged 18 and over in Saddleworth who subscribed to the Protestation in February 1641/2. **No person surnamed Bentley or Bently appears on the Saddleworth return.**
   - **Historical context:** Only three years earlier, at the West Riding Quarter Sessions in **January 1638/9**, **Mathewe Bentley**, late of Saddleworth, labourer, was ordered committed to **York Castle** for refusing to become bound with sureties to appear at the next sessions (*SHS Bulletin* 21(1), Spring 1991, p. 12; see [research/2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md](2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md)). If the 1613–14 father or his 1614 son was imprisoned, absent from the chapelry, or living outside the parish in February 1641/2 before **"Mathey" Bentley** was buried at St Chad's on **8 February 1657/8**, that absence would explain why no Bentley signed the 1641/2 Saddleworth Protestation.

2. **The Saddleworth Hearth Tax Returns (1664–1674):**
   - Analysed and transcribed across all four townships (Quick, Lordsmere, Shawmere and Friarmere) by Mike Buckley in *"The Saddleworth Hearth Tax Returns"* (Parts 1–3), *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*, Vol. 31, No. 3 (Autumn 2001), pp. 11–15 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Vol-31-No-3-Autumn-2001.pdf)); Vol. 32, No. 1 (Spring 2002), pp. 17–21 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Vol-32-No-1-Spring-2002.pdf)); and Vol. 33, No. 2 (Summer 2003), pp. 6–13 ([PDF](https://www.saddleworth-historical-society.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Vol-33-No-2-Summer-2003.pdf)).
   - **No Bentley appears in the chargeable Hearth Tax returns for Saddleworth.**
   - **Historical context:** We know from the St Chad's register that **Jonas Bentley** was resident in Saddleworth in **August 1654** and **December 1664**, and from `H/KID p. 248` that he died **"of Standedge, Saddleworth"** in **July 1686**. His absence from the chargeable Hearth Tax lists of 1664–1674 demonstrates that his household at Standedge was either exempt on grounds of low assessment/poverty or held as an under-tenancy whose hearth was answered for by the immediate landlord or manor owner.

### 4.2 Distinguishing John Bentley of Diggle (d. 1713/14) from John Bentley of Boarshurst (d. 1750)

In the early eighteenth century, two different men named **John Bentley** left probate records in Saddleworth. Separating them prevents conflating two distinct branches of the Saddleworth Bentley network:

### 4.3 Pushing back to the 1500s and 1400s: a border-wide audit across Saddleworth, Rochdale/Spotland and the West Riding (1379–1680)

To test whether the Saddleworth Bentleys (beginning in the surviving St Chad's registers with **Mathew Bentley** in **1613–14** and **Jonas Bentley of Standedge** in **1654–86**) can be linked to a documented **fifteenth- or sixteenth-century** Bentley line, we audited the primary tax, muster, manorial, parish and probate records across three zones: **(1) inside Saddleworth before 1613**, **(2) East of the Pennines (Halifax/Calderdale, Almondbury and the York Probate Registry)**, and **(3) West of the Pennines (Rochdale/Spotland and the Chester Probate Registry)**.

#### 4.3.1 Inside Saddleworth before 1613 (1379–1612)

- **1379 West Riding Poll Tax (*Quick / Saddleworth*):** No person surnamed Bentley or *de Benteley* appears in the Quick return ([research/2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md](2026-10-10-saddleworth-line.md)).
- **15th- and 16th-century Saddleworth subsidies, musters and manor deeds (*SHS Bulletin* survey):** A comprehensive check of fifteenth- and sixteenth-century Saddleworth records published in the *Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin*—including the **1524/1545 Tudor Lay Subsidies**, the **1539 Saddleworth Muster Roll**, and Tudor Byron/Ramsden manorial conveyances—shows **no occurrence of the surname Bentley in Saddleworth in the 1400s or 1500s** (when local records are dominated by foundational medieval Saddleworth surnames such as Platt, Gartside, Buckley, Shaw, Broadbent, Nield, Whewall and Radcliffe).
- **1612 Bishop's Transcript vs. 1613 Parish Register:** As shown in [research/2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md](2026-11-04-origins-and-limits.md), no Bentley baptism, marriage or burial occurred during the single year of the surviving 1612 diocesan return, before **Mathew Bentley** baptised his daughter **An** on **1 May 1613** and his son **Mathew** on **2 December 1614**.

#### 4.3.2 East of the Pennines: Halifax, Shelf, Heptonstall, Sowerby, Almondbury and the York Probate Registry (1275–1619)

In the Calder Valley (Halifax parish, Morley Wapentake), the surname *de Benteley* / *Bentley* is documented from the late thirteenth century onward (referencing High Bentley / Low Bentley in Shelf). However, auditing the fourteenth- to early seventeenth-century records reveals a decisive onomastic and documentary boundary between the Halifax/Shelf Bentleys and the seventeenth-century Saddleworth Bentleys:

1. **Complete Absence of *Matheus/Mathew* and *Jonas* from the 1538–1593 Halifax Parish Registers and the *Calderdale Companion*:**
   - A check of the name indexes to E. W. Crossley's *The Parish Registers of Halifax, Co. York* (**Vol. I / YPRS Vol. 37**: Baptisms, 1538–1593; **Vol. II / YPRS Vol. 45**: Marriages and Burials, 1538–1593) confirms that **neither *Matheus* / *Mathew* nor *Jonas* appears under the surname Bentley anywhere in Halifax parish between 1538 and 1593**.
   - Likewise, Malcolm Bull's *Calderdale Companion* ([`MMB122.html`](https://calderdalecompanion.co.uk/mmb122.html), indexing 154 Bentley biographical entries across Calderdale) contains **no main biographical entry for a Matthew Bentley or Jonas Bentley**.
   - Thus, quite apart from the two chronological breaks that disproved the old Ancestry tree's link through Staups House (Shibden) and Elland Hall ([research/2026-10-10-the-two-breaks.md](2026-10-10-the-two-breaks.md)), the characteristic seventeenth-century Saddleworth given names **Mathew** (1613, 1614, 1664) and **Jonas** (1654, 1664, 1686) are completely absent from the sixteenth-century Halifax/Shelf registers.
2. **Almondbury Parish Register (1557–1652, YAS PRS Vols. 139 & 140):**
   - The ancient parish of **Almondbury** (All Hallows) included **Marsden** on the eastern side of Standedge. While the post-Reformation given names *Mathew* and *Jonas* were used in Almondbury parish, no baptism or marriage in the free transcripts yet links the 1613 Saddleworth **Mathew Bentley** or the 1654 **Jonas Bentley of Standedge** to an Almondbury father.

#### 4.3.3 West of the Pennines: Rochdale/Spotland and the Diocese of Chester (1400s–1680)

Because St Chad's, Saddleworth, was a chapelry of the **Parish of Rochdale** (in the **Diocese of Chester**), the closest fifteenth- and sixteenth-century Bentley family in the same ecclesiastical parish lived immediately west of Saddleworth at **Woodhouse in the township of Spotland (Rochdale)**:

1. **The Bentleys of Woodhouse in Spotland, Rochdale Parish (1400s–1650):**
   - In *A History of the County of Lancaster: Volume 5* (Victoria County History, *Townships: Spotland*, [British History Online](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/vch/lancs/vol5/pp199-213)) and Lieut.-Col. Henry Fishwick's *The History of the Parish of Rochdale in the County of Lancaster* (1889) and *The Survey of the Manor of Rochdale, 1626* (Chetham Society, New Series, Vol. 71, 1913), **Woodhouse** in Spotland is documented as having been **"for several centuries the residence of a family named Bentley"**:
     - **Early 16th century:** **William Bentley of Woodhouse**.
     - **Mid-to-late 16th century:** **Arthur Bentley, yeoman, of Woodhouse**, who married **Elizabeth**, daughter of **Thomas Chadwick of Healey Hall**; she was buried at Rochdale on **19 May 1588**.
     - **Early 17th century:** **William Bentley of Woodhouse** married **Jane Midgley** (daughter of the Puritan vicar of Rochdale, Rev. Joseph Midgley) at Rochdale Church on **2 January 1602/3** and was buried at Rochdale on **27 February 1604/5**. By the **1626 Rochdale Manor Survey**, **Arthur Bentley** (alongside Samuel Greave, who paid quit-rents to the King, the suppressed Priory of St John, and the Holts of Stubley) held land at Woodhouse.
     - **1639 & 1650 Chester Wills:** The Chester probate index for 1621–1650 (below) records the wills of **Jane Bentley of Woodhouse Lane in Spotland (1639)** and **Arthur Bentley of Woodhouse Lane (1650)**.
   - **Evaluation:** Geographically and ecclesiastically, Spotland in Rochdale parish is the closest sixteenth-century Bentley seat to Saddleworth. However, the Woodhouse family consistently used the forenames **William** and **Arthur**, and no record in Fishwick's Rochdale registers (1582–1641) or the 1626 Manor Survey names a **Mathew** or **Jonas** Bentley.

2. **Complete Audit of Bentley Probate Entries at Chester, 1621–1680 (*RSLC* Vols. 4 & 15):**
   - Before **Jonas Bentley of Standedge's 1686 administration and inventory** (*RSLC* Vol. 18, p. 291; `H/KID`, p. 248), the printed Chester probate indexes for **1621–1650** (*Record Society of Lancashire and Cheshire*, Vol. 4, ed. J. P. Earwaker, 1881) and **1660–1680** (*RSLC*, Vol. 15) list the following **17 Bentley estates** across Lancashire and Cheshire (none in Saddleworth):
     - **Lancashire entries (1621–1650, *RSLC* Vol. 4):**
       - **Bentley, George**, of Colne, maltman (**1637**)
       - **Bentley, Jane**, of Woodhouse Lane in Spotland, Rochdale parish (**1639**)
       - **Bentley, Mary**, of Manchester (**1639**)
       - **Bentley, Arthur**, of Woodhouse Lane [Spotland] (**1650**)
     - **Cheshire, Derbyshire & unlocalised entries (1621–1650, *RSLC* Vol. 4):**
       - **Bentley, Randle**, of Coysley, co. Chester (**1625**); **Bentley, Lady**, of Bredsall Park, co. Derby, widow (**1628**); **Bentley, Alice**, of Meire, widow (**1633**); **Bentley, William**, of Hulse, co. Chester, husbandman (**1633**); **Bentley, George**, of Meire, yeoman (**1637**); **Bentley, George**, of the Meyre (**1639**); **Bentley, Richard**, of Hulse, co. Chester, yeoman (**1642**); **Bentley, Alice**, widow (**1650**); **Bentley, Arthur**, of Knutsford (**1650**); **Bentley, Richard**, of the Meire, co. Chester, Inv. (**1650**).
     - **1660–1680 (*RSLC* Vol. 15):**
       - **Bentley, William**, M.D., of Northwich, Admon with Inv. (**1670 / 1680**); **Bentley, John**, of Meyre [Meir], Inv. (**1674**); **Bentley, William**, of Hulse, Inv. (**1675**).

3. **Other Seventeenth-Century West Riding Comparators Audited and Excluded:**
   - **Jonas Bentley, B.A., Clerk, Curate of Baildon (fl. 1635–1638):** Documented in W. Paley Baildon, *Baildon and the Baildons* (Vol. I, p. 363; Vol. III, p. 97) as curate of Baildon Chapel in 1635–1638; an university-educated clergyman in Wharfedale/Airedale, distinct from Jonas Bentley of Standedge (d. 1686).
   - **Matthew Bentley of Leeds, slaymaker (married 1675/6):** Documented in *Paver's Marriage Licences, Vol. III, 1674–1714* (YAS RS Vol. 46, 1912, p. 17), aged 23 in 1675/6 when licensed to marry Mary Hutton of Leeds; distinct from Mathew Bentley of Saddleworth (bap. 11 Dec 1664, wife Ellin).
