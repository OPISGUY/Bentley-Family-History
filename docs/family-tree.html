How far back does the evidence reach?

The earliest identified local Bentley appears in 1613, but three unresolved steps prevent calling this an unbroken ancestry. Read the origins and limits and the 11 November Standedge-to-1600s evidence review for the missing relationships, record losses, seventeenth-century tax and legal checks, and new probate and map evidence. The earliest appearance is not proof of first arrival.

Boxes: green means the person is found in records; yellow means found in records, but part of how they join the line is still inference (see the table below). Arrows: solid green means a record names the parent; dashed means probable, not yet proven.

The free-source review found a St Chad's baptism transcription naming William and Betty as Charles's parents on 8 June 1828, at Standage, father a clothier. This strongly matches Charles in the previously recorded 1851 household.

The remaining gap is which William: identifying Charles's father with William baptised on 8 April 1788 to John and Ann Bently remains probable. On 6 June 1819, William and Betty Bentley of Standage, clothier, and James and Ann Bentley of Standage, clothier, brought their sons William and Charles to be baptised at St Chad's on the exact same Sunday—matching the names, hamlet and trade of the two younger surviving brothers (James, bap. 1784, and William, bap. 1788) of John and Ann's family, and introducing the forename Charles that William and Betty also gave our ancestor in 1828. Because neither the 1815 marriage entry nor the baptisms explicitly name William's father, the chart continues to separate the two Williams as a probable identity.

The family is now documented at Standedge even earlier than previously thought: the Leonard Kidd probate abstract (H/KID, p. 248) of Jonas Bentley's 18 August 1686 inventory explicitly describes him as "of Standedge, Saddleworth", forty years before the parish registers first name the farm in 1726. Deeds at Oldham Archives (D-AAB/1/1–1/5) show two Bentley clothiers—James Bentley of Blakehey Nook and John Bentley of Bentleys / Standedge—buying the freehold tenement called "Bentleys" from the lord of the manor in 1791 and selling it in 1806, while the 1822 Map of Saddleworth marks BENTLEYS next to STANEDGE FOOT on the Standedge turnpike, distinct from Bleak Hey Nook lower down the hill. See the 11 November Standedge-to-1600s review and the Saddleworth line.

Other family lines to explore

Each marriage brings in another family. The Haigh and Platt branches now have findings; the others remain open:

  • Cartwright: The 1934 Huddersfield birth indexed with mother Mayall is a candidate. Margaret’s exact birth date, later married surname and death registration are still open.
  • Jones: Lauretta Jones, born 1887/8, Barkisland (Halifax district).
  • Haigh: Sarah Ann Haigh (c.1857–10 August 1934), daughter of Thomas and Harriet Haigh in the Marsden memorial index. Dan Haigh (c.1820) is an uncle in the 1891 household; his precise relationship to her parents needs checking.
  • Platt: Helen Bentley's family with Thomas Platt, including seven children and a further generation; see family groups.
  • Dyson: Sarah Dyson, born about 1831, Marsden.
  • Bottomley: Betty Bottomley, born about 1782, Saddleworth.

New date evidence and conflicts

Lauretta's preferred working death date is 25 September 1963, supported by a public newspaper OCR notice naming Thomas Herbert as her husband. John's likely memorial gives 10 November 1918, but its age 67 conflicts with his 1861 birth evidence; a nearby Marsden memorial for Sam Bentley (husband of Rose Hannah, died 14 December 1966, age 82) shows a similar age discrepancy if he is John's son born about 1890. In Australia, Melbourne newspaper notices on Carlisle Street, St Kilda now verify 12 October 1891 for William's death and 27 July 1902 for Wright's death, and link Joseph's two St Kilda households (with Irvine Nisbett and Ann Jones) to one father at 66 Carlisle Street. See the 4 November source review and the 10 November Binn and Carlisle Street evidence.

Earlier research: the Ancestry tree and High Bentley Hall

This project began by testing an Ancestry family tree that traced the family back to John G. Bentley of High Bentley Hall, Shelf, near Halifax (died 1540), and beyond to Monmouth. Checked against the records, that tree turned out to join several unrelated families together, so it has been set aside. Its correct parts (Charles Bentley onwards) match the line above.
