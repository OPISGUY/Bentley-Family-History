Saddleworth · Standedge · Marsden · St Kilda (1613–1998)

More than names on a parish page.

Most genealogy sites flatten human lives into boxes and dates. This chronicle rebuilds eleven generations of the Bentley family from free parish registers, wills, manor court rolls, maps and newspapers—following the work of their hands from a 1613 Saddleworth christening and a 1686 Standedge farmhouse to the stone quarries and woollen mills of Marsden and three brothers’ houses on Carlisle Street in St Kilda.

Era I · 1613–1686

The 17th-Century Pennine Frontier

Only three loose annual register sheets survive at St Chad’s before 1632, capturing Mathew Bentley christening his daughter An (1613) and son Mathew (1614). A 1619–20 Manchester hue and cry and a January 1638/9 committal order to York Castle trace the name through turbulent decades before Jonas Bentley’s 1686 inventory places the family on the ridge at Standedge.

Era II · 1686–1835

The Yeoman Clothiers of Standedge

Across five generations on the Standedge escarpment—at Deanhead, Knowl, Barn, Meerleech, Bleak Hey Nook and the two-cottage freehold called “Bentleys” beside Standedge Foot—the family wove woollen cloth while a Diggle kinsman worked as a carpenter. On Sunday, 6 June 1819, brothers William and James Bentley stood side by side at St Chad’s font to christen their sons.

Era III · 1851–1918

Marsden Stone & Melbourne Sea

In 1851 widowed Betty Bentley headed a cottage of four woollen-weaving sons at Carr Clough, Marsden. Within a decade their paths split across the world: Joseph, William and Wright remade their neighbourhood at 62–66 Carlisle Street, St Kilda, while Charles stayed in Marsden, putting down the shuttle to quarry stone at Binn while ten-year-old John pieced wool in the mill below.

Era IV · 1891–1998

Below the Reservoir Embankment

As Huddersfield Corporation demolished Upper Bank Bottom Mill (“Th’ Top Bonk”) in 1891 to build Butterley Reservoir, John and Sarah Ann (née Haigh) raised their children at Binn alongside Uncle Dan Haigh. Their son Thomas Herbert married Lauretta Jones in 1920, farmed in Slaithwaite with his son Roy, and ended his life in a terrace on Binn Road below the water.

Place 1 of 15 · 1613–1835 St Chad’s, Uppermill · Saddleworth

St Chad’s Church, Saddleworth

Generations here: Mathew & An (1613–14) · Jonas (1654–86) · Old Matthew & Ellin (1700–35/6) · Helen (“Ellin”) Platt (1769 gravestone) · Brothers William & James at the font (6 June 1819)

Founded in 1215 as a moorland chapelry of St Chad, Rochdale, this hillside church is the cradle of the surviving Bentley record. On 1 May 1613—on only the second surviving annual sheet of St Chad’s register—Mathew Bentley christened his daughter An here. Here “old Matthew” of Stone-edge was buried in February 1735/6, here Helen Platt’s 1769 gravestone still spells her name “Ellin”, and here on Sunday, 6 June 1819, brothers William and James Bentley of Standage stood side by side at the font to christen their sons William and Charles.

Primary Archival Evidence

St Chad’s Diocesan Transcripts (1613–14); Parish Registers (1632–1835); Ellin & Thomas Platt Gravestone in the Old Churchyard (1769 / 1789, Kidd 1994, p. 4); Double Baptism of 6 June 1819.

1888/9–1965  ·  Weaver’s son · Binn Road below Butterley

1861–1918  ·  Woollen piecer (age 10) → Woollen weaver

Partner · Sarah Ann Haigh

Born at Carr Clough in early 1861 while his father and Aunt Ann wove woollens at home, John was already working in a Marsden mill at ten years old, piecing together broken wool threads while his fifteen-year-old sister Elizabeth wound bobbins. Baptised 1664  ·  Hillside householder (“old Matthew”)

Partner · Possibly Ellin

Christened on 11 December 1664 as the younger son of Jonas Bentley, he was twenty-one when his father died at Standedge in 1686. Jonas Bentley

Died 1686  ·  Smallholder at Standedge (1686 Inventory)

The earliest Bentley documented on the high ridge of Standedge by name. Born during the 1615–1631 gap in St Chad’s loose diocesan transcripts, Jonas baptised his elder son John in August 1654 (who fits the Diggle carpenter of 1713/14) and—after the burial of old “Mathey” Bentley in February 1657/8—named his next recorded son Mathew in December 1664. His 18 August 1686 probate inventory places him “of Standedge, Saddleworth” forty years earlier than the parish registers.

Mathew Bentley

Recorded in 1613–14  ·  Earliest recorded Saddleworth Bentley father

On 1 May 1613—on only the second surviving annual sheet of St Chad’s register after a 67-year local blackout—Mathew Bentley brought his daughter An to be christened in Saddleworth, followed nineteen months later by a son Mathew on 2 December 1614. Across 73 years (1613–1686), Mathew and Jonas of Standedge are the only two Bentley fathers recorded anywhere in Saddleworth, while a 1619–20 Manchester hue and cry and a January 1638/9 committal order to York Castle preserve dramatic traces of the name before the Civil War.

The 1500s and 1400s boundary and trans-Pennine corridors: No Bentley appears in 15th- or 16th-century Saddleworth subsidies (1524, 1545), the 1539 Muster Roll, or the 1379 Poll Tax for Quick. Across the entire lan-opc.org.uk database for St Chad, Rochdale (1582–1750) and St Mary, Oldham (1558–1790), as well as E. W. Crossley's Parish Registers of Halifax (1538–1593, Vols. 37 & 45) and Malcolm Bull's 154 Calderdale Companion Bentley entries (MMB122.html), there is not a single 16th- or 17th-century baptism of a Mathew or Jonas Bentley, confirming that the Mathew / Jonas line belonged specifically to Saddleworth and further separating it from High Bentley Hall in Shelf. 1482–1686 · The 1613 Horizon & 3-Point Link to Jonas

Why Mathew didn’t come from thin air

St Chad’s registers before 1632 are lost save for three loose sheets (1612–1614) after a 67-year blackout since 1545. Born in the 1580s when Elizabethan families first adopted biblical names like Mathew and Jonas, Mathew of 1613–14 links forward to Jonas of Standedge (d. 1686) through three facts: the 1615–31 Bishop’s Transcript gap matches Jonas’s birth window; Mathew and Jonas are the only two Bentley fathers in Saddleworth across 73 years (1613–86); and Jonas named his next son Mathew in 1664 after “Mathey” Bentley was buried in February 1657/8.

26 Nov 1791 & 1822 · Standedge Foot & Woodmans

Where was “Bentleys” — and who shared Woodmans?

The 1822 Map of Saddleworth marks “BENTLEYS” next to “STANEDGE FOOT” on the turnpike road (where co-owner John Bentley of Bentleys lived on 26 November 1791), distinct from Bleak Hey Nook (where co-owner James Bentley lived). On that exact same day in November 1791, the two-dwelling farmhouse called Woodmans was occupied jointly by Matthew Bentley (the 1738/9 uncle) and Thomas Rhodes (father of niece Esther Bentley’s 1779 child).
