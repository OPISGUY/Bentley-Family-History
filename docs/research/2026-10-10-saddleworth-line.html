The Saddleworth line, 10 October 2026

Working back from William Bentley (married 1815) through the Saddleworth Bentleys, using free online sources only: the FreeREG transcript of the St Chad, Saddleworth registers, the printed Chester probate indexes, The National Archives' catalogue (Discovery) and the 1379 poll tax.

Earlier work: the line rebuilt from records (Roy back to William) and the two breaks (the first pass through the Saddleworth registers).

Summary

1. William's baptism has been found, free, on FreeREG. On 8 April 1788 the Saddleworth register records the baptism of William, son of John and Ann Bently, of Meerleech, the father a clothier. Earlier searches missed it because the clerk spelled the name "Bently" from 1788 to 1792. John and Ann (née Broadbent, married 1771) were the only John and Ann Bentley having children at Saddleworth between 1765 and 1800. William's parents are now documented. 2. One question remains at this point in the line: is the William baptised in 1788 the William who married Betty Bottomley in 1815 and fathered Charles? Very probably (see below), but no single record yet says so. 3. Burials found under "Bently": John of Standedge, William's father, on 15 November 1789. A James Bently of Standedge, clothier, on 8 February 1790, probably William's grandfather. 4. Wills. The Chester probate indexes list a will for Matthew Bentley of Standedge, clothier (proved 1753), administration of the goods of Jonas Bentley of Saddleworth (1686), and a will for a James Bentley of "Standage" (1792). 5. The family's home at Standedge was called "Bentleys". A run of deeds at Oldham Archives follows a house and about seven and a half acres near Standedge, "known as Bentleys", from 1791 to 1896. Two Bentley clothiers bought it from the lord of the manor's trustees in 1791 and sold it in 1806. 6. Before 1613. The Saddleworth registers begin in 1613. No Saddleworth Bentley appears in the Chester probate indexes from 1545 to 1680, or among the people taxed in Saddleworth (the township of Quick) in 1379. The family probably came to Saddleworth between 1379 and 1613. That is as far back as free sources reach for now.

1. William Bentley, baptised 1788

The record. FreeREG, Saddleworth St Chad (transcript by John Lacey, file line 1004): baptised 8 April 1788, William, son of John and Ann Bently, abode Meerleech, father's occupation clothier (FreeREG).

The Ancestry tree gives the same date and the place "Meerlech". Its date for John's death (15 November 1789) also matches the register exactly (section 2). So the tree's facts for this family seem to have been taken from the registers themselves.

Why the record was missed before. Searches for "BENTLEY" find nothing at Saddleworth between June 1787 and July 1793. In those years the clerk wrote "Bently". A search with spelling variants switched on brings up 16 baptisms and 17 burials under "Bently" for 1785–1793. The spelling turns up now and then from the 1760s too. All Saddleworth Bentley entries from 1600 to 1800 have now been searched with spelling variants switched on. Nothing new turned up before 1765.

Only one John and Ann. Every Bentley baptism at Saddleworth from 1765 to 1800 was checked with spelling variants. Only one couple was named John and Ann, and only one John Bentley married an Ann (Ann Broadbent, 2 April 1771; FreeREG). Their children:

Is this the William who married Betty in 1815? Very probably:

  • He is the only likely candidate. Besides John and Ann's two Williams, two other William

    • Bentleys were baptised at Saddleworth between 1765 and 1800. "Billy", son of John and Sarah of Delph, was baptised in 1786 and buried in 1789. The other was baptised on 4 April 1779: William, son of Thomas Rhodes of Warrockhill and Esther Bentley, "spinster of Standedge" (FreeREG). He would have been 35 in 1815, and may have been known as William Rhodes. Esther was probably John's sister (see section 3).

  • His age. The Ancestry tree gives William's death as 16 April 1835 at Ridge, Saddleworth,

    • aged 47, which fits 1788, not 1779. The burial record itself hasn't been seen: Saddleworth burials after 1800 aren't transcribed online.

  • Place. William and Betty's sons were born at Standedge ("Stanage" 1821, Standedge 1831,

    • according to the Ancestry tree), where John and Ann had lived.

  • Names. William and Betty's eldest son was John, after William's father.

What would settle it: William's burial in 1835 (with his age), Charles's baptism in 1828, or Charles's 1855 marriage certificate (it names his father). See the research plan.

5. "Old Matthew" (1664–1736), Jonas (died 1686) and Mathew (fl. 1613)

The register entries are listed in the two breaks. New evidence:

  • Jonas Bentley's death is confirmed. The Chester index has "Bentley, Jonas, of Saddleworth

    • … Admon with Inv 1686" (index, 1681–1700 volume): letters of administration of his goods, with an inventory. This matches "Jonah Bentley", buried 6 July 1686. An inventory lists a person's goods room by room, and would show what kind of household Jonas kept.

  • No probate for "old Matthew". The 1721–1740 index has no Saddleworth Bentley, so he

    • probably left no will.

  • John Bentley of Diggle, died 1713. The Chester index has "Bentley, John, of Saddleworth,

    • carpenter … Admon with Inv 1713" (index, 1701–1720 volume). West Yorkshire Archive Service (Leeds) has a 20th-century copy of the will of "John Bentley of Diggle (1713)", in a small collection of Saddleworth wills (ACC 2433). He may be Jonas's elder son John, baptised in 1654, and so old Matthew's brother. That is not proven.

  • Abraham Bently of Standedge was buried on 23 February 1792 aged 84 (born about 1708)

    • (FreeREG). An Abraham Bentley was having children at Dowry-greens, Stone-edge and Acre in 1740–1759. Abraham may be a younger son of old Matthew, and Matthew of Standedge's brother.

How these men relate is unchanged from the first pass. Mathew, baptised 1664, son of Jonas, is probably "old Matthew". Old Matthew is probably the father of Matthew of Standedge, since they lived on the same farm. Jonas's own father is not known. He may be a son of the Mathew of 1613–14.

6. Bentley wills in the Chester indexes

Saddleworth's church came under the Diocese of Chester, so its wills were proved at Chester. The Record Society of Lancashire and Cheshire printed indexes of these wills, and the volumes are on archive.org. Every volume from 1545 to 1820 was searched. Entries for Saddleworth Bentleys:

1753Bentley, Matthew, of Standedge in Saddleworth, clothier1741–1760
1785Bentley, Jane, of Kinders, Chapelry of Saddleworth, widow … Admon.1781–1790
1792Bentley, James, of Standage, County of Lancaster1791–1800
1795Bentley, Henry, of Saddleworth fould1791–1800

"Admon" means letters of administration, granted when there was no will. Entries without it are wills. Henry of Saddleworth Fold (buried 25 June 1795) and Jane of Kinders belong to other Saddleworth Bentley families.

No Saddleworth Bentley appears in the volumes for 1545–1620, 1621–1650, 1660–1680, 1721–1740, 1761–1780, 1801–1810 or 1811–1820. From 1653 to 1660 all wills went to a single court in London. No Saddleworth Bentley was found among those in The National Archives' catalogue.

Where the originals are. Saddleworth was part of Rochdale parish, in the Lancashire half of the Diocese of Chester. The original Chester wills for Lancashire are at Lancashire Archives in Preston (Manchester Archives guide). Copies can be ordered for a fee. Matthew's will (1753) and James's (1792) are the two most useful for this line.

7. "Bentleys", the family home at Standedge

Oldham Archives holds a bundle of deeds (reference D-AAB) for "the property called Bentleys": a house and about 7½ acres near Standedge, beside the turnpike road from Delph to Marsden. Later deeds describe it as two cottages at Rough, near Standedge. The deeds came from a firm of Chesterfield solicitors, by way of Derbyshire Record Office. The earliest deeds involve the family:

DateDeedCatalogue
5 Nov 1791James Bentley of Blackay Nook, clothier, to John Bentley of Bentleys, clothier: "all that messuage or tenement … known as Bentleys in Saddleworth" with the closes called the Meadow, the Old Meat, the Far and Near Sutake and the Croft (about 7½ acres), a newly built house at Standedge Foot, and land on the north side of the turnpike road from Delph to Marsden. £30D-AAB/1/1
25–26 Nov 1791The heir and trustees of James Farrer of Barnborough Grange (lord of the manor of Saddleworth) sell Bentleys to John Bentley of Bentleys and James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothiersD-AAB/1/2
1–2 Dec 1791John Bentley of Bentleys mortgages it to John Wood of Cleckheaton for £200D-AAB/1/3
10 Aug 1792John Bentley of Standedgefoot, clothier, and John Wood transfer the mortgage to John Battye of Crosland HillD-AAB/1/4
7–8 Jul 1806Sold, at the direction of John Bentley of Standedge, clothier, with James Bentley of Blake-hey-nook, clothier, to Joseph Scholefield, merchant. £350 and £240D-AAB/1/5

Later owners included the Wrigleys of Castleshaw (1818–1845), Henry Cheetham, James Dyson of Barkisland (1885), George Richards (1893) and Sarah Buckley of Delph (1896) (collection description).

What it means. A farm known simply as "Bentleys" had been in Bentley hands long enough to take the family's name. This branch had lived at Standedge (also written Stone-edge) since at least 1726, and "old Matthew" was there before 1736. Until 1791 the Bentleys held the farm from the lord of the manor. James Farrer, the lord, had died by then (the deed names his heir and the trustees of his will), and in 1791 the manor's lands were sold, much of it to the sitting tenants (Saddleworth Historical Society bulletin). The Bentleys bought theirs that November.

Who were John and James? Not yet certain:

  • James Bentley of Blakehey (Bleak Hey) Nook, clothier, 1791 and 1806, is probably the James

    • who, with his wife Matty (Martha), had children baptised in 1787–1793, at Bleak Hey Nook in 1791 and 1793 (FreeREG). He may be James, son of James and Mary, baptised at Stone-edge in 1761: William's uncle.

  • John Bentley of Bentleys (1791) and of Standedgefoot (1792). This can't be William's

    • father, who died in 1789. Nor can it be William's brother John, who was 10. He was another member of the family, not yet identified.

  • John Bentley of Standedge (1806) may be William's brother John (baptised 1781), then 25 and

    • the eldest surviving son of John of Standedge.

The deeds themselves (free to see at Oldham Archives) may name heirs and relationships that the catalogue doesn't give.

Bentley Farmhouse. A Grade II listed, mid-18th-century farmhouse called Bentley Farmhouse stands at Bleak Hey Nook, near Standedge (Historic England list entry 1068123). James Bentley lived at Bleak Hey Nook in 1791–1806. Whether it is the "Bentleys" of the deeds is not yet known.

8. Before 1613

  • The Saddleworth registers begin in 1613, with the bishop's transcripts from 1612. The

    • earliest Bentley in them is Mathew, whose daughter An was baptised on 1 May 1613.

  • Probate, 1545–1650: no Saddleworth Bentley in the Chester indexes.
  • The 1379 poll tax: the return for Quick (the old name of the township of Saddleworth)

    • lists more than 50 taxpayers, with married couples taxed together. None is a Bentley (GENUKI transcription). They include Shaws, Fernlees, Wrigleys, Platts, Lees and Brierleys, surnames still found in Saddleworth centuries later.

So the Bentleys probably came to Saddleworth between 1379 and 1613. Bentley was a common surname in the Calder valley and in south-east Lancashire. No record yet shows where this family came from or when.
