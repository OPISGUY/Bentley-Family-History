Earlier Bentley line: searches and new leads

5 November 2026. Free public sources; no accounts or purchases.

Result

This round has not established another parent–child link. The earliest identified local Bentley remains Mathew, a father in 1613. His relationship to Jonas is unknown. The results below narrow specific searches and identify collateral households to investigate; they are not additional proven ancestors.

The missing 1696 entries

Mike Buckley's “Addendum to Saddleworth Parish Registers 1696”, SHS Bulletin 49(1), 2019, pp. 32–34, publishes entries omitted from Radcliffe's edition. I checked the full published baptism, marriage and burial lists on pp. 32–33: no Bentley appears. This particular supplement does not supply the younger Matthew's missing baptism or his parents' marriage.

The article describes omissions in both the original registers and bishops' transcripts. Consequently, an unsuccessful index search is not enough to conclude that a baptism never happened. The supplement covers a single register year, not all the missing years around Matthew's proposed birth.

Read the supplement, PDF p. 36.

Neighbouring Rochdale marriages

I searched the Lancashire Online Parish Clerks transcription for St Chad, Rochdale, marriages 1683–1700, including the Bentley/Bently stem. The Bentley entry found was John Bentley and Sara Birchley, 11 August 1684. The transcription supplies no abode or parentage for them. It therefore cannot identify John as Jonas's son, and does not provide the sought Matthew–Ellin marriage.

This is a result for this transcription and date range only. It does not exclude marriages elsewhere or in other years. Transcription, citing the original register.

Mary Bentley of Standedge: a collateral lead

The Oaken Hill gazetteer records 1 February 1781: John Hall, clothier of Oakenhill, married Mary Bentley of Standedge. Source: SHS Bulletin 5(3), p. 53, citing the registers. I visually checked the printed page, including the year, rather than relying on OCR column order.

Mary, baptised in 1756 to James and Mary Bentley, is a candidate for this bride: the chronology and locality are compatible. The bride's parents are not named in this extract, so no identity or marriage has been attached to that daughter yet. The original marriage, witnesses, subsequent children's baptisms and burials are the next checks. A separate John Hall–Mary Bentley marriage at Oldham in 1749/50 must not be substituted for this one.

Gazetteer, PDF p. 19.

Matthew Bentley at Woodmans: a later man

An advertisement dated 26 October 1784, reproduced from the Manchester Mercury, names Matthew Bentley among occupants of land at Woodmans near Delph, as under-tenants of Charles Scanlon. Source: SHS Bulletin 2(2), pp. 22–23; the Woodmans gazetteer on p. 34 repeats the sale reference.

This is too late to identify the occupant as Matthew of Standedge who died in 1751. Matthew baptised in 1738/9 is a possible candidate, but the advertisement does not establish his age or parents. Keep this as a separate identity lead. Reproduced advertisement, PDF p. 6.

The surviving farmhouse does not date the family's arrival

Historic England dates the listed Bentley Farmhouse on Whimberry Lee Lane to the mid eighteenth century. Its listed fabric therefore cannot itself prove that the 1613 family lived in that building. An earlier dwelling on the site remains a separate question for deeds and archaeology. Official listing 1068123.

What can actually establish the earlier links?

The highest-priority surviving documents remain Jonas Bentley of Standedge's 1686 administration and inventory and Matthew of Standedge's 1751 will, proved 1753. Copies are catalogued in the Leonard Kidd collection, H/KID, p. 248. I have located the catalogue descriptions, not inspected the documents themselves. No freely readable full copy was located in this search round. A catalogue hit is not evidence of the heirs' names.

An administration bond may identify relatives or associates; an inventory may identify appraisers and property. Neither necessarily names a father. Matthew's will may help distinguish his children and property, but need not mention his deceased parents. These are promising tests, not guaranteed answers.

SHS archive catalogue. See also the existing early-line evidence and gaps.
