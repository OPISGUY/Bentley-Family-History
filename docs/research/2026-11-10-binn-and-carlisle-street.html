Binn workplaces, Marsden memorials and Carlisle Street, St Kilda

10 November 2026. Free public sources; no accounts or purchases.

Summary of findings

1. Three Bentley households on Carlisle Street, St Kilda: Digitised Melbourne newspapers in the National Library of Australia's Trove archive place the households of William Bentley (at 62 Carlisle Street), Wright Bentley (at "Stockwell", Carlisle Street) and Joseph Bentley (at "Baroona", 66 Carlisle Street) on the same street in St Kilda, Victoria. 2. Exact Australian death dates verified: Contemporary family notices independently verify William Bentley's death on 12 October 1891 (in his 66th year, husband of Hannah) and Wright Bentley's death on 27 July 1902 (in his 71st year, husband of Helen). 3. The two St Kilda Joseph Bentley households shared one father: The 27 November 1915 Argus death notice for Ann Bentley at "Baroona", 66 Carlisle Street, St Kilda, widow of Joseph Bentley, names "Arch." (Archibald, born 1859 to Joseph Bentley and Irvine Nisbett) first among her children alongside David (1866), Mrs. R. O. Scott, Mrs. J. Armstrong (Dorothy Jane, 1870), H. J. Bentley (Henry Joseph, 1876) and Mrs. W. Bloomfield. This establishes that Joseph Bentley who married Irvine Nisbett in 1854 and Joseph Bentley who married Ann Jones in 1865 were the same man. 4. Two Joseph Bentleys of Saddleworth distinguished: A 2013 Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin article states that the educational writer Joseph Bentley, author of Gems of Biography during Infancy, Boyhood and Youth (1856), was born at Stanedge in 1821. Cross-checking the 2006 Bulletin and biographical records of the school inspector Joseph Bentley (1805–1872) shows that the memoirist was born in Saddleworth in 1805, not 1821. His 1856 Delph school recollection must not be attached to Betty Bentley's son Joseph (baptised 16 September 1821). 5. Binn in 1881 and the mills below Binn Road: The Marsden History Group's 1881 census analysis for Binn records 18 households and 78 people, naming both Bentley and Haigh among the resident families and listing 2 stone quarrymen, 2 stone masons, 4 weavers, 1 weaver and farmer, 1 piecer and 1 person living on their own means. Local histories from the Marsden History Group and Kirklees Council also show why Upper Bank Bottom Mill ("Th' Top Bonk"), where Dyson and Co. advertised in November 1889, disappears from later records: Huddersfield Corporation demolished it in 1891 to build Butterley Reservoir, and its site lies beneath the far end of the reservoir embankment. 6. Additional St Bartholomew's Marsden memorials: Reading the full Bentley block in the St Bartholomew's graveyard index adds six more entries, including Sam Bentley (died 14 December 1966, age 82), his wife Rose Hannah Bentley (died 13 November 1938, age 57) and their daughter Ethel Bentley (died 29 April 1915, age 6).

Earlier searches recorded Victorian death-index transcriptions for William (1891 / 18081), Wright (1902 / 11699) and Joseph (1907 / 06379) at St Kilda, naming William and Betty (Bottomley) as parents, but kept the candidate households of Joseph and Irvine Nisbett, Joseph and Ann Jones, and William and Hannah Thorpe separate pending connecting evidence (5 November Australian note).

Status of connecting the William/Hannah Thorpe household to Betty's son William: Supported across death registration, age, spouse name and shared street address with Wright and Joseph.

2. Two Joseph Bentleys of Saddleworth: avoiding a published conflation

In Shelby Shrigley, “A Delph School From The Inside”, Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin, 43(1), Spring 2013, p. 20 (PDF), the author writes:

> “James may have attended the school run by ‘Old Betty’ in Delph. We know of this from the autobiography of Joseph Bentley. He was born, the son of handloom weavers, at Stanedge, above the Castleshaw valley, in 1821. He was four years old and still in petticoats but recalls that at the school ‘...we were crowded together in a very small room, a sort of cellar, lower than the street; that we were rough and noisy, with scarcely a book amongst us, only a few odd leaves of any book we could get...’”

Taken at face value, that passage would appear to give our Joseph Bentley (baptised 16 September 1821 at St Chad's, abode Standage, son of William and Betty) a published childhood memoir and an account of attending "Old Betty's" dame school in Delph.

Cross-checking the source cited on page 25 of the same Bulletin issue ("An Outline History of Saddleworth Schools", note 6) and in SHS Bulletin, 36(1), Spring 2006, p. 13 ("A Plate-Layer's Wife", note 1) shows why this must be rejected:

1. The quotation comes from Joseph Bentley, Gems of Biography during Infancy, Boyhood and Youth (London, 1856, pp. 74–75). 2. The author of Gems of Biography was the well-known nineteenth-century educational reformer, statistician and self-styled “Oldest School Inspector”, Joseph Bentley (1805–1872), who was born in Saddleworth in 1805, lost his employment after speaking in support of the 1832 Reform Bill, worked on Pigot's directories, and spent his adult life campaigning and publishing in England. 3. By 1854–56, when Gems of Biography was published in London, our Joseph Bentley (baptised 1821) was in Victoria, where his marriage to Irvine Nisbett (1854 / 01499) and the birth of their son William at St Kilda (1855 / 01308) were registered. 4. The 2013 Bulletin article appears to have attached the 1821 Standage baptism date to the 1805 Saddleworth educational reformer. We record this here so that the 1856 autobiography is not mistakenly added to our Joseph's life story.

3. Binn in 1881 and the mills below Binn Road

Two open questions in the current research plan concern the working landscape around Binn, Bank Bottom and Carr Clough in Marsden, and the relationship between the textile mills at Bank Bottom and the building of Butterley Reservoir.

Binn in the 1881 census

The Marsden History Group's settlement study for Binn (marsdenhistory.co.uk/binn.php) analyses the 1881 census for the hamlet, which lies midway between our 1871 and 1891 census returns:

  • Size of the hamlet: 18 households and 78 people in total (average household size 4.3; 34 residents listed as employed).
  • Resident family surnames listed: Flint, Sykes, Hawley, Hall, Warham (servant), Lumb, Haigh, Manchester, Lumley, Whiteley, Bentley, Bamforth, Kinder, Firth and Carter.
  • Occupations across the 18 households: 5 farmers, 1 weaver and farmer, 4 weavers, 3 fettlers, 2 warpers, 1 piecer, 1 woollen knotter, 1 shawl twister, 1 teaser, 1 woollen merchant, 1 box mender, 2 stone quarrymen, 2 stone masons, 1 iron planer, 2 pupil teachers, 1 auctioneer's clerk, 1 tailor's apprentice, 1 gamekeeper, 2 servants and 1 person living on their own means.

This confirms that both Bentley and Haigh households were present at Binn in 1881 (two years before John Bentley married Sarah Ann Haigh in 1883), and shows how closely the hamlet's 34 working residents combined hillside farming, stone quarrying/masonry and woollen mill work. The page gives aggregate figures and surnames rather than individual household schedules, so the exact 1881 census transcription for Charles and John Bentley is still worth locating when FreeCEN access allows.

What happened to Upper Bank Bottom Mill ("Th' Top Bonk")

Earlier notes cited the London Gazette of 29 November 1889 (p. 6867), recording a partnership change for Dyson and Co. at Upper Bank Bottom Mill, Marsden, and the London Gazette of 22 November 1889 (p. 6396), proposing Butterley Reservoir.

Local histories from the Marsden History Group (Upper Bank Bottom Mill) and Kirklees Council (Swellands walking route Marsden, PDF) explain how those two notices connect:

  • Upper Bank Bottom Mill, known locally as “Th’ Top Bonk”, stood on the Wessenden Brook and was already mapped in 1801. By 1851–69 it was a three- and four-storey scribbling, spinning and weaving mill powered by a 28½-foot waterwheel (with a 27-foot fall) and steam engine, operated by Ben Sykes & Son until Ben Sykes's bankruptcy in 1869.
  • Demolition for Butterley Reservoir: In 1891—the very year of John and Sarah Ann Bentley's census return at Binn—Huddersfield Corporation demolished Upper Bank Bottom Mill in order to use its site for the construction of Butterley Reservoir. As the Kirklees walking guide notes, the site of Upper Bank Bottom Mill now lies buried underneath the far end of the Butterley Reservoir embankment.

Lower Bank Bottom Mill, Ottiwells Mill and Clough Lee Mills

The Marsden History Group's mill profiles clarify the other workplaces immediately below Binn Road and Carr Clough:

1. Lower Bank Bottom Mill (marsdenhistory.co.uk/lower-bank-bottom-mill.php): Shown on the 1801 township map and 1854 Ordnance Survey sheet beside Bank Bottom Bridge. Rented by John Crowther and Sons (Joseph, William and Elon Crowther) from 1867/1870 until 1901, when Huddersfield Corporation purchased it; the Corporation demolished it in 1913 to use the site as a storehouse during the Butterley Reservoir works. 2. Ottiwells Mill and the new Bank Bottom Mill (marsdenhistory.co.uk/ottiwells.php; marsdenhistory.co.uk/bank-bottom-mill.php): Ottiwells Mill (built 1793, famous during the Luddite disturbances of 1812 under William Horsfall) stood at the bottom of Binn Road beside Ottiwells Bridge over Wessenden Brook. In 1876, the Crowthers' Marsden Mill Co. invited tenders for a new five-storey woollen mill (60 × 30 yards) on the Ottiwells site. Its chimney (St Thomas) and engine (Hopeful) were named in 1878, and by 1901 this expanding complex at the foot of Binn Road had taken the name Bank Bottom Mill under the Crowther family (later John Edward Crowther Ltd). 3. Clough Lee Mills (marsdenhistory.co.uk/clough-lee-mills.php) and Carr Clough (Turnpike routes leaflet, PDF): For the 1851 and 1861 Bentley households at Carr Clough, the nearest mill in the clough was Clough Lee Mills, rebuilt in 1841 and expanded in 1860 by Robinson Bros (managed for nearly fifty years by Richard Beaumont). The Marsden History Group's turnpike study also notes that the older line of Mount Road formerly crossed Carr Clough and its stream by a bridge or culvert before being superseded.

These records do not name individual Bentley employees, so we still do not assign Charles, Ann, Elizabeth or John to a specific firm. They do, however, establish the exact mills at the foot of Binn Road and Carr Clough and show that one of the two Bank Bottom mills disappeared beneath Butterley Reservoir while Thomas Herbert Bentley was a small child at Binn.

Additional Marsden leads: the 1886 petition and Binn Lodge Farm

  • 1886 Church Lane Widening Petition (marsdenhistory.co.uk): Signed by 200 ratepayers and inhabitants of Marsden in 1886. The transcribed signatories include Sykes Dyson (Lower Bank Bottom Mill) and John Bentley (Planks). Because our John Bentley (born 1861) was at Binn in 1871 and 1891, the Planks signatory may be another man (for example, the older John Bentley whose memorial gives age 67 in 1918, or a cousin). Keep John Bentley of Planks distinct until census or directory evidence clarifies whether there were two adult John Bentleys in Marsden.
  • Binn Lodge Farm (marsdenhistory.co.uk/farming.php): In its account of Marsden farming between 1900 and 1950 (note 43), the Marsden History Group records a Bentley woman at Binn Lodge Farm making butter to supplement wartime rations. Because we have not yet verified a death date for her, we omit her given name here under the repository's privacy rule while recording Binn Lodge Farm as a documented twentieth-century Bentley farm address at Binn.

5. Standedge field names and the 1788 "Meerleech" abode

William Bently's 8 April 1788 baptism transcription gives John and Ann's abode as Meerleech ("Meerlech" in the old tree). A search across the Saddleworth Historical Society Bulletin gazetteer and property notes found no settlement headword under that spelling, but identified two relevant local features near Standedge and Bleak Hey Nook:

1. Wimberlee / Whimberry Lee: Deeds and gazetteer entries for Knowle, Standedge Foot and Bleak Hey Nook record closes called Great Wimberlee and Little Wimberlee, alongside Whimberry Lee Lane (part of the 1760 turnpike route, where the listed mid-eighteenth-century Bentley Farmhouse stands). 2. Boundary and water elements (meer/mere and leech/leach): In northern Pennine place and field names, meer/mere denotes a boundary or pool and leech/leach a boggy stream or rushy ground, while cursive Wimberlee / Meerleech can also be misread in register transcripts.

This does not prove that Meerleech is a variant or misreading of Wimberlee, but it narrows the question to a specific check on the original 8 April 1788 register entry at St Chad's.
