Bentley households in Victoria: children and unresolved connections

Research checked 5 November 2026

The following are documented households in public transcriptions of Victorian registration indexes. The underlying certificates have not been inspected. A child's entry names parents; it does not, by itself, establish that the father was Charles Bentley's brother. These are separate research households until that identity is established. All registration numbers below refer to Victoria, Australia.

Hannah Bentley's death 1901 / 15624, St Kilda, age 66, names father John Thorpe and mother Mally Wrigley. It is a likely identification of the mother above, not proof of her husband's parentage.

Archibald's and Anne's 1859 births have consecutive registration numbers at Pt Nepean. That is useful context for researching the households together, not evidence that their fathers were brothers. The 1858 death places William and Hannah's household in the Victorian records by then; it cannot yet date the migration of Charles's brother.

Search coverage and limits

Public birth transcription pages examined cover 1851–1890, including the intermediate two-year pages. The page labelled 1861–1870 actually contains 1861–62; the page labelled 1871–1875 contains 1871–72. These titles must not be mistaken for complete coverage. No matching Wright/Ellen birth was found in the examined 1881–90 transcription pages; that is not evidence that they had no children.

Marriage pages examined: 1851–70 and 1881–1900. Death transcription pages examined: 1851–1912. This is a surname-led index search, not an exhaustive search of every married daughter's descendants. Unrelated Bentley households are excluded. No paid certificates, accounts or subscriptions were used.

The useful next evidence is a free probate file, death notice, grave inscription or passenger record explicitly connecting a spouse/child to one of the men whose parents were William and Betty. A matching name and town alone is not enough. The two Joseph households must not be merged merely because their dates allow a remarriage.
