What comes next for the family stories

The people we could still hear about first-hand

The family has supplied the first personal detail for Roy and Margaret’s story. Both remarried. Roy’s later wife was Betty. Margaret had already died before the reporting relative was born. The relative was six when Roy died. Margaret’s later married surname would help identify the right death entry. Her exact dates remain open.

Binn and the Slaithwaite farm

Thomas’s family identifies the terrace below the reservoir as the place where he died. The Marsden History Group’s 1900–50 farming notes also record a twentieth-century Bentley farming connection at Binn Lodge Farm, where homemade butter supplemented wartime rations. A historical directory or electoral register pairing Thomas or Roy with a named Slaithwaite farm—or giving the house number of Thomas’s terrace on Binn Road—would help date the moves. Roy continuing at the farm and Thomas returning to Binn remain plausible recollections to test. Read the family account · Read the 10 November Binn evidence.

A day at work in Marsden

For Charles, Ann, Elizabeth and John, the censuses tell us exactly what jobs they did. We can now map the workplaces immediately below their homes: Clough Lee Mills (Robinson Bros) below Carr Clough; Upper Bank Bottom Mill (“Th’ Top Bonk”, where Dyson and Co. advertised in November 1889 before Huddersfield Corporation demolished the mill in 1891 to build Butterley Reservoir over its site); Lower Bank Bottom Mill (rented by John Crowther and Sons from 1867/1870 until 1901 and demolished in 1913 as a reservoir storehouse); and the Crowthers’ five-storey Bank Bottom Mill begun in 1876 on the Ottiwells site at the foot of Binn Road. An 1881 census profile for Binn also counts 18 households and 78 residents, naming both Bentley and Haigh among the resident families and listing two stone quarrymen, two stone masons, four weavers and a piecer. The next question is which specific mill or quarry employed each Bentley, and whether the 1881 Binn census schedules can be transcribed in full. Read Ann’s story · Read the Carlisle Street story.

A wartime story needs a name and a household

Thomas Herbert was of serving age in 1914. A free record connecting a service number to his address or a known relative would give us a basis for a military story. His service history remains open. Read the newspaper and military notes.
