Family groups

Last updated: 11 November 2026

This is the populated companion to the direct-line chart. It includes siblings and the newly researched Haigh and Platt branches. It is not a claim that every child or spouse has been found. Living and possibly living people are excluded.

D = documented in the cited record, often through a transcription; S = supported identification or secondary-source reconstruction; F = family claim awaiting a record. A record naming parents documents that relationship, but matching those parents to an earlier couple can still be uncertain. Approximate census ages are not exact birth dates.

Sources used throughout:

  • R: October reconstruction, with census and civil-index references. Carried forward, not all freshly rechecked.
  • SAD: October Saddleworth research, with parish citations.
  • CW: William/Charles baptism review.
  • NEW: November wider-family evidence, containing exact queries, source locations and conflicts.
  • BCS: 10 November Binn, Marsden and Carlisle Street evidence, with Trove newspaper notices, Bright Cemetery entry and Marsden mill/hamlet records.
  • S16: 11 November Standedge-to-1600s evidence, with H/KID probate abstracts, 1822 Saddleworth map evidence, the 6 June 1819 double baptism and seventeenth-century parish/manor checks.

1. Roy Bentley and Margaret Cartwright

Roy (1930–1998) married Margaret Cartwright, registered October–December 1953, Upper Agbrigg, 2d/989. Roy's parents are Thomas Herbert Bentley and Lauretta Jones. Margaret's parents remain unidentified; Mayall is the mother's maiden name in a probable birth-index match, not a proven maternal identity. Source: R.

Later marriages (family account): Roy later married Betty. Margaret also remarried. Her later spouse’s name and the dates of the remarriages are unknown here. A relative reports that Margaret died before their birth and that they were six when Roy died. The Huddersfield death registered under Margaret Bentley in 1991 remains an unconfirmed candidate. Her exact birth date previously came from that same entry.

Later generations are private and deliberately absent.

Baptisms are at St Chad's; father a clothier in the earlier entries. On 6 June 1819, William and Betty Bentley of Standage, clothier, and James and Ann Bentley of Standage, clothier, brought their sons William and Charles to be baptised at St Chad's on the exact same Sunday—corroborating the probable identification of William (husband of Betty) and James (husband of Ann) with the surviving brothers James (bap. 1784) and William (bap. 1788) in Group 7, and introducing the given name Charles that William and Betty also gave our ancestor in 1828. The three William entries must not be collapsed into one person or assigned infant deaths without burial evidence. Harry remains a separate candidate because of the mother's apparent age and possible delayed baptism.

1851: Betty, widow, age 69, Carr Clough, HO107/2291 f.133 p.9. Sources: R, CW, S16.

In November 1791 (D-AAB/1/1–1/2) and May 1806 (D-AAB/1/5), two Standedge clothiers—James Bentley of Blakehey Nook, clothier, and John Bentley of Bentleys / Standedge, clothier—bought and sold the freehold tenement called "Bentleys" (8 acres in Lordsmere). The 1822 Map of Saddleworth (SHS Bulletin 29(1), Spring 1999, p. 8) marks "BENTLEYS" and "STANEDGE FOOT" at the foot of the Standedge escarpment near Burk Clough and Thieves Bridge, distinct from Bleak Hey Nook lower down the hill where Grade II listed Bentley Farmhouse stands and where co-owner James Bentley lived. Meanwhile, on 6 June 1819, James and Ann Bentley of Standage, clothier, and William and Betty Bentley of Standage, clothier, baptised their sons Charles and William at St Chad's on the same day, supporting the identification of James and William with the surviving sons baptised in 1784 and 1788 above. Source: S16.

9. Matthew Bentley of Standedge and Sarah

Matthew, clothier, buried 20 November 1751; will dated 18 November 1751, proved 3 March 1753 (H/KID, p. 248). Sarah's surname is unknown: “Joanes” from the earlier tree has not been established. Clara Shaw's Knowl gazetteer (SHS Bulletin 12(1), Spring 1982, p. 14) notes that prior to 1724 Matthew and Sarah lived at Deanhead before moving to Knowl and Standedge (where an unplaced Martha Bentley, spinster of Knowl, also had a daughter Mary baptised on 14 July 1744). The link to old Matthew remains probable, supported by a four-point residence and naming correlation (continuous Standedge abode, wife Ellin → daughter Helen/Ellin, children Ester 1700 and John 1702/3 → children John 1728/9 and Esther 1731, and first son born after old Matthew's 1735/6 burial named Matthew in 1738/9). 10. Helen Bentley and Thomas Platt

The connection to Matthew and Sarah's daughter and the marriage on 5 January 1753 are supported by Kidd's published reconstruction (SHS Bulletin 24(3), Autumn 1994, pp. 3–4). Kidd records that Helen Platt died on 1 September 1769, in her 36th year—one week after her youngest son Joseph was baptised—and was buried at St Chad's on 9 September 1769 (followed by baby Joseph on 17 November 1769). Her age at death ("in her 36th year" on 1 September 1769) independently matches her 20 January 1733/4 baptism at Knar, and her gravestone at St Chad's (with husband Thomas Platt of Whams Inn, who died 9 September 1789 and was buried 12 September 1789) inscribes her name as Ellin. Neither the 1641/2 Saddleworth Protestation Return (Radcliffe 1887, p. 483) nor the 1664–1674 Saddleworth Hearth Tax Returns (SHS Bulletin 31(3), 32(1), 33(2)) lists a Bentley—consistent with Mathewe Bentley's January 1638/9 York Castle committal order before 1641/2 and with Jonas holding at Standedge as an exempt smallholder or under-tenant in 1664–74. In the early eighteenth century, John Bentley of Diggle, carpenter (Admon with Inventory 18 February 1713/14, H/KID, p. 248; WYAS Leeds ACC 2433), is a chronological candidate for Jonas's 1654 son John in the hamlet immediately adjacent to Standedge, whereas John Bentley of Boarshurst, yeoman (executor in 1703, bondsman in 1712, joint tenant of Frenches Fulling Mill in the 1719 Manor Rental, will dated 13 August 1743 / proved 12 May 1750, and named with his mother Elizabeth on the 13 June 1750 Saddleworth Manor Court Roll) headed a separate documented branch at Boarshurst.

The 1500s and 1400s boundary: No Bentley appears in 15th- or 16th-century Saddleworth subsidies (1524, 1545), the 1539 Muster Roll, or the 1379 Poll Tax for Quick. East of the Pennines, a check of E. W. Crossley's Parish Registers of Halifax (1538–1593, Vols. 37 & 45) and Malcolm Bull's 154 Calderdale Companion Bentley entries (MMB122.html) reveals zero occurrences of the given names Matheus/Mathew or Jonas under the surname Bentley in 16th-century Halifax/Shelf, further separating the Saddleworth family from High Bentley Hall and Shibden. West of the Pennines, in the same Mother Parish of Rochdale, the Bentleys of Woodhouse in Spotland held land across the 15th, 16th and 17th centuries (VCH Lancaster Vol. 5; wills of Jane Bentley 1639 and Arthur Bentley 1650 in RSLC Vol. 4), using the forenames William and Arthur. No descent from High Bentley Hall is asserted, and no primary record yet names the father of Mathew (fl. 1613–14) or Jonas (d. 1686). Full 11 November 1400s–1800s analysis.
