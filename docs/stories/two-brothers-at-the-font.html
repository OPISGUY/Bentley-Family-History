William, Betty, James and Ann Bentley · Standage, Coat Hill and Ridge, 1789–1835

Two brothers at the font on a June Sunday

When John Bentley of Standedge was buried at St Chad’s in November 1789, aged thirty-nine, he and his wife Ann Broadbent had already endured the heartbreak of burying three infant sons within two days of their baptisms. Three boys survived him on the hill: John, who was eight; James, who was five; and little William, who had been christened at Meerleech only nineteen months before his father’s death.

What became of those fatherless boys as they grew into men? In the registers of the early nineteenth century, where clerks recorded a father’s trade and hamlet but not his own parents’ names, following a younger son across the gap between childhood and marriage is one of the hardest tasks in family history. At Standage, the trail comes alive through two clothiers’ households that moved in step with each other.

In August 1808, James Bentley married Annamina Broadbent—called Mimy and Ann in the registers—at St Chad’s. Six and a half years later, on 5 January 1815, William Bentley married Betty Bottomley at the same church. Both couples made their homes at Standage, and both husbands earned their living as woollen clothiers.

Then came Sunday, 6 June 1819. On that summer day, William and Betty Bentley of Standage and James and Ann Bentley of Standage walked to St Chad’s Church together and stood at the font on the same day. William and Betty had their baby boy christened William. Right beside them, James and Ann had their baby boy christened Charles—the very first time the name Charles ever appeared in the Standedge Bentley family. Nine years later, on 8 June 1828, William and Betty gave that exact same name—Charles—to our ancestor at Standage.

Read in a table, those entries are dates and parishes. Pictured in life, they are two brothers who had lost their father in the autumn of 1789 standing shoulder to shoulder as master clothiers at the parish font thirty years later, sharing each other’s family names. By 1831 William and Betty were at Coat Hill, where Wright was baptised; by the spring of 1835 they were at Ridge, where William died at forty-seven and Betty returned to St Chad’s in November as a widow to christen baby Harry, before gathering her weaving sons and crossing the moor into Marsden.
