John Bentley and Ann Broadbent · Standedge, 1771–89

A name given again

In August 1776 John and Ann gave a son his father’s name. He was baptised on the twenty-first and buried on the twenty-third. The following August they called another son John. Again, just two days lay between baptism and burial. When a little William was baptised in July 1779, his burial followed two days later too.

Three boys survived: John, baptised in 1781, James in 1784, and another William in April 1788. Two years after their father John was buried at Standedge in November 1789, aged about thirty-nine, two Bentley clothiers—James of Blakehey Nook and John of Bentleys—bought the freehold of the family tenement called “Bentleys”, mapped in 1822 right beside Standedge Foot on the turnpike road.

The youngest boy, William, had been only nineteen months old when his father died. Yet thirty-one years later, on Sunday, 6 June 1819, two Standage clothiers named James Bentley and William Bentley stood at St Chad’s on the very same day to have their sons baptised—James and Ann naming their boy Charles, and William and Betty naming theirs William, before giving their own younger son the name Charles in 1828. That shared Sunday at the font is the closest glimpse we have of the two brothers standing side by side as grown men.
