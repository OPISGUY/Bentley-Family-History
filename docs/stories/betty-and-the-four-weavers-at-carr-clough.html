Betty Bentley and her children · Carr Clough and St Kilda, 1851–1907

Four weavers under Betty’s roof

John, William, Charles, Wright. In 1851 all four men lived with their widowed mother Betty at Carr Clough, and all four gave the census taker the same answer about their work: woollen weaver. Their sister Ann was eighteen. It was quite a household to find together at one address.

The family had already made a life on both sides of Standedge. When Charles was baptised in 1828, his father William was a clothier at Standage. Wright’s baptism three years later gives Coat Hill. By the time Betty’s grown sons were counted under her roof in Marsden, their father had died.

Charles would stay in the district. For William, Wright and another brother, Joseph, the trail leads to St Kilda in Australia. The most telling detail comes at the end of Wright’s life. In 1902 his death entry there gave his mother’s name as Betty Bottomley. The ships and the farewells are missing from this story. What survives on the far side of the world is the name of the woman with whom those four weavers once shared a home.
