William, Wright and Joseph Bentley · St Kilda, Victoria, 1854–1950

Three houses on Carlisle Street

When the census taker came to Carr Clough in the spring of 1851, Betty Bentley had four grown sons weaving wool under her roof. Ten years later only Charles was still there. The trail of three brothers—Joseph, William and Wright—runs half a world away to the bayside suburb of St Kilda, outside Melbourne. What the Victorian death indexes did not show is how close together they lived when they got there.

The family notices in the Melbourne newspapers give the street. On 12 October 1891 William Bentley died at his home in Carlisle Street West, St Kilda, in his sixty-sixth year. Ten years later his widow Hannah died at 62 Carlisle Street. Their eldest surviving son, John William, lived on in that same house at number 62 until his own death in February 1950.

Seven months after Hannah’s death, another notice appeared in the Melbourne papers. On 27 July 1902 Wright Bentley died at his residence, “Stockwell”, Carlisle Street, St Kilda, in his seventy-first year, the husband of Helen Bentley. In the Victorian death register the informant went one generation further back, naming Wright’s parents as William Bentley and Betty Bottomley.

Two doors from number 62 stood a house called “Baroona”, at 66 Carlisle Street. That was the home of Joseph and Ann Bentley. Joseph died at St Kilda in 1907, aged eighty-five. When Ann died at “Baroona” in November 1915, her death notice named six children: Arch., David, Mrs R. O. Scott, Mrs J. Armstrong, H. J. Bentley and Mrs W. Bloomfield. Naming Arch. first settles an old puzzle. Archibald had been born at Point Nepean in 1859 to Joseph’s first wife, Irvine Nisbett, who died two years later. By listing Irvine’s son beside her own children, Ann’s notice shows that the two St Kilda households belonged to one father.

We still do not know which ship carried each brother out from England, or which of them first took a house on Carlisle Street and drew the others to the same road. What the notices preserve is the neighbourhood they remade. Decades after leaving Saddleworth and Marsden, three sons of William and Betty were living within doors of one another on a single street in St Kilda.
