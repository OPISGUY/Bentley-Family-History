John Bentley and Sarah Ann Haigh · Marsden, 1861–1934

The boy who stayed at Binn

There are two views of John at Binn, twenty years apart. In the first he is ten, a piecer in the woollen trade, living with his father Charles and his mother Sarah. His older sister Elizabeth winds bobbins. In the second, taken in 1891, he is thirty. The enumerator writes him down as a woollen weaver, an employee, and head of the household.

John had married Sarah Ann Haigh in 1883. By that second census there were four young children at home: Willie, six; Sarah, four; Thomas Herbert, two; and Sam, one. There was also Dan Haigh, a seventy-one-year-old uncle. The household ranged from a one-year-old to a man old enough to remember a very different Marsden.

Sarah Ann lived until August 1934. On her memorial at St Bartholomew’s, she is named both as John’s wife and as the daughter of Thomas and Harriet Haigh. Anyone who starts out looking only for a Bentley will find a Haigh family there too.
