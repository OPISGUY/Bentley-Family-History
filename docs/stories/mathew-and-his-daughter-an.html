Mathew Bentley and his daughter · Saddleworth, 1613–14

Her name was An

Two letters were enough for the parish clerk: An. On the first of May 1613, a little girl of that name was baptised at St Chad’s in Saddleworth. Beside hers went the name of her father, Mathew Bentley. Four centuries later, it is the daughter who gives us our first glimpse of him.

A boy followed. On 2 December 1614 another Bentley child was baptised, this time with his father’s own name. For those children the hills were home. Somewhere among the households of Saddleworth were two people who could both answer to Mathew Bentley, one old enough to be a father, the other newly baptised.

Years later a Mathew Bentley of Saddleworth was wounded by a man called Henry Platte. Word travelled as far as the Manchester constables, who recorded the hue and cry. Was the injured man An’s father? All that joins them is a name and a place. The part of her father’s life we can recognise with confidence is quieter. Before a Bentley ever made the constables’ book, An had already put one in the parish register.
