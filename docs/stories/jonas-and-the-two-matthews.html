Jonas, John and Mathew Bentley · Standedge, Saddleworth, 1654–1736

Eleven days in August

John Bentley was eleven days old when he was baptised. He had been born on 16 August 1654, a detail someone thought worth preserving alongside the date of his baptism on the twenty-seventh. His father was Jonas. Ten winters later Jonas had another son baptised, a December boy called Mathew.

By July 1686 Jonas himself was gone. A burial entry calls him Jonah, and an inventory of his goods made on 18 August 1686 describes him as Jonas Bentley of Standedge, Saddleworth. That single line in the probate papers places the family on the Standedge hillside forty years earlier than the parish registers first name the farm. His sons, if both were still living, would then have been about thirty-one and twenty-one—and a John Bentley of neighbouring Diggle, carpenter, whose estate was inventoried in February 1713/14, fits the elder brother's years.

The younger boy carried the same name as a Bentley father recorded in Saddleworth half a century before him, and the same name as the “old Matthew” buried from Stone-edge in 1735/6. Whether Jonas belonged to the 1613 household is still an open question. At Standedge, though, he now has a clear place: the earliest Bentley we can put on that high ridge by name.
