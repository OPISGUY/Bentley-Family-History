Jonas, John and Mathew Bentley · Standedge, Saddleworth, 1654–1736

Eleven days in August

John Bentley was eleven days old when he was baptised. He had been born on 16 August 1654, a detail someone thought worth preserving alongside the date of his baptism on the twenty-seventh. His father was Jonas. Ten winters later Jonas had another son baptised, a December boy called Mathew.

By July 1686 Jonas himself was gone. A burial entry calls him Jonah, and an inventory of his goods made on 18 August 1686 describes him as Jonas Bentley of Standedge, Saddleworth. That single line in the probate papers places the family on the Standedge hillside forty years earlier than the parish registers first name the farm. His sons, if both were still living, would then have been about thirty-one and twenty-one—and a John Bentley of neighbouring Diggle, carpenter, whose estate was inventoried on 18 February 1713/14 (four and a half months before Mary Bentley of Saddleworth married by licence to James Bradbury of Fairbanks, Diggle, clothier, on 3/10 July 1714 and named her firstborn son John), fits the elder brother's years.

The younger boy carried the same name as the Bentley father recorded in Saddleworth half a century before him, and the same name as the “old Matthew” buried from Stone-edge in 1735/6. Even without a surviving baptism for Jonas himself, three facts bind him to Mathew of 1613–14: Jonas was born during the 1615–1631 gap when St Chad's loose diocesan transcripts are missing and Mathew was having children; Mathew and Jonas are the only two Bentley fathers recorded anywhere in Saddleworth across seventy-three years (1613–1686); and after "Mathey" Bentley was buried at St Chad's on 8 February 1657/8, Jonas gave his next recorded son the name Mathew in December 1664. At Standedge, Jonas stands as the bridge between the 1613 register and the eighteenth-century clothiers of the ridge.
