Mathew Bentley and Ellin · Stone-edge, 1700–1736

The man they called old Matthew

Somewhere along the way one of the Matthews of Stone-edge became old Matthew. It was the name by which the parish clerk distinguished him when he was buried in February 1735/6. It suited a hillside where a younger Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge was already raising a family of his own.

Earlier entries offer glimpses of the older household. Ester, daughter of a Mathew, was baptised in September 1700. A daughter of Mathew was buried in December 1702, her name left unwritten. A boy called John was baptised in February 1702/3. In May 1719 Ellin was buried, remembered as the wife of Mathew Bentley. When the younger Matthew and his wife Sarah named their own children at Stone-edge and Knar in the 1720s and 1730s, they chose John, Esther and Helen—written Ellin on her gravestone—and then, in the first birth of a boy after old Matthew was laid to rest in 1735/6, they named a son Matthew.

Jonas of Standedge had a son Mathew baptised in 1664. If he was the man the clerk later called old Matthew, he lived past seventy on the same ridge where his father's goods were inventoried in 1686. No single baptism survives from the gap-ridden 1690s to name the younger Matthew as his son, yet the farm they shared and the names carried forward into the next generation tell a remarkably coherent story.
