# The clothier at Knoll

Matthew and Sarah Bentley · Saddleworth, 1722–51

When James was baptised in March 1722, his parents, Matthew and Sarah, lived at Dean-head. Their daughter Mary’s entry in January 1723/4 gives Knowll, on the western slope of Standedge, and by the time Sarah was baptised in 1726 the place written beside her name was Stone-edge. Not far below Deanhead, at Acre, another clothier of their generation, Abraham Bentley, was working in 1750 before ending his eighty-four years at Standedge. A whole life could be spent within a mile or two of these hills and still leave a string of different farm names in the register.

Matthew was a clothier. Cloth made in Saddleworth could travel over the hills by packhorse to Huddersfield. The Pennines look rather less like the edge of the world when you picture a load of woollen cloth going to market.

At home the years brought births and losses, and the names chosen for the children echoed the older Bentley household on the hill: John in 1728/9, Esther in 1731, Helen in 1733/4. In February 1735/6 “old Matthew” of Stone-edge was buried; that April Matthew and Sarah baptised Anna, and in July they buried their seven-year-old son John. When their next boy arrived in January 1738/9, they called him Matthew. Alice and Betty followed. When their father made his will on 18 November 1751 and was buried two days later, the two details the clerk chose for him were his home and his trade: Matthew Bentley of Stone-edge, clothier.
