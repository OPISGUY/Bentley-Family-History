# The boy who stayed at Binn

John Bentley and Sarah Ann Haigh · Marsden, 1861–1934

There are two views of John at Binn, twenty years apart. In the first he is ten, a piecer in the woollen trade, living with his father Charles and his mother Sarah. His older sister Elizabeth winds bobbins. In the second, taken in 1891, he is thirty. The enumerator writes him down as a woollen weaver, an employee, and head of the household.

John had married Sarah Ann Haigh in 1883. Both surnames had already been counted among the eighteen households at Binn in 1881. By that 1891 census there were four young children at home: Willie, six; Sarah, four; Thomas Herbert, two; and Sam, one. There was also Dan Haigh, a seventy-one-year-old uncle. The household ranged from a one-year-old to a man old enough to remember a very different Marsden.

Right below Binn, the valley floor was about to be remade. In November 1889 Dyson and Co. had advertised a change in partnership at Upper Bank Bottom Mill—known locally as “Th’ Top Bonk”—in the same month that Huddersfield proposed damming the valley for Butterley Reservoir. In 1891, the year the census taker visited John and Sarah Ann, Huddersfield Corporation demolished Upper Bank Bottom Mill so that the new reservoir embankment could rise over its site. Lower Bank Bottom Mill and the Crowthers’ five-storey mill at Ottiwells, right at the foot of Binn Road, carried on.

Sarah Ann lived until August 1934. On her memorial at St Bartholomew’s, she is named both as John’s wife and as the daughter of Thomas and Harriet Haigh. The same churchyard index records a Sam Bentley, husband of Rose Hannah, who died in December 1966, though its transcribed age leaves a question to test against the one-year-old at Binn. Anyone who starts out looking only for a Bentley will find a Haigh family—and a whole hillside community—there too.
