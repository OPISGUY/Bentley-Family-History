# The man they called old Matthew

Mathew Bentley and Ellin · Stone-edge, 1700–1736

Somewhere along the way one of the Matthews of Stone-edge became *old Matthew*. It was the name by which the parish clerk distinguished him when he was buried in February 1735/6. It suited a place where a name could belong to several men within walking distance of one another.

Earlier entries offer glimpses of a household that may have been his. Ester, daughter of a Mathew, was baptised in September 1700. A daughter of Mathew was buried in December 1702. The entry leaves her unnamed. A boy called John was baptised that winter too. In May 1719 Ellin was buried, remembered as the wife of Mathew Bentley.

Jonas had a son Mathew baptised in 1664. If he was the man the clerk later called old Matthew, he lived into his seventies. There is room for that life in the dates, but the surviving entries never quite tie the two ends together. What the burial gives us, across all that distance, is something a modern family might recognise: a familiar name with the one extra word everybody needed to know who was meant.
