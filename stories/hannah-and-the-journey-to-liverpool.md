# One woman, two places to remember her

Hannah Platt Gartside · Rochdale and Liverpool, 1788–1833

Hannah was born into a family that worked both cloth and land. Her father, James Platt, farmed and manufactured cotton. Her grandmother was Helen Bentley of Heyhouse. In 1810 Hannah married John Gartside, a bookkeeper who had been baptised as a grown man in the river Roche. He went on to serve as a Baptist deacon. The couple made homes at Haugh and then at Hamer, near Rochdale.

At forty-five, Hannah was in Liverpool. Why she had gone there is one detail her family history has lost. What it preserves is the date: 6 August 1833. She died of cholera and was buried at St James’ Mount in Liverpool.

Back at Ogden, her name was remembered on a gravestone. A relative looking for Hannah there might reasonably think she lay beneath it. Her story belongs to both places: the city where she died and the community that made room for her name back home.
