# The other weaver in the house

Ann Bentley · Carr Clough, 1851–61

Baby John was three months old when the census taker came to Carr Clough in 1861. He was too young to have known what his aunt did for a living. We know because the man who counted the household wrote it down. Ann Bentley, his father’s sister, was a woollen weaver.

Ten years earlier Ann had been eighteen and living with her widowed mother Betty. Four of her brothers were weavers. It would be easy to tell that as a story about the Bentley men and pass over the youngest person in the house. The next census puts Ann alongside her brother Charles in the same trade, living under the same roof with his wife Sarah and their children.

We can follow the baby into adulthood, to a house and children of his own. Ann slips from this account much sooner. But at twenty-eight she had a family relationship, a home and an occupation all recorded on the same line. For that one April day at least, she comes into view as plainly as her brother.
